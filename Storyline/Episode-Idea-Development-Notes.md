## 1. The Crossing (2564)

**What canon already has**
- **The founding.** Palmer City was settled on 2564-06-21, the Treaty's day, by robots, their human partners and supporters, "united by their relationship to robots rather than by nationality" (GL Palmer City; SL `Worldspace/Locations.md`).
- **The Treaty.**
  - Robots are exiled as a class; humans who go are deemed to have "willingly and permanently relinquished" personhood (SL `Locations.md`, Title IV).
  - Maggie Aarden signs at Stanley under Assembly pressure.
  - Janbogo's pre-Treaty archives are wiped hours before the signing (SL `Storyline/The-Falkland-Treaty.md`).
- **Theme material.** Legal personhood was "never the same thing as being treated as if it were true" (SIP, Theme Stated).

**Gaps**
- Who led the landing, and where the ships sailed from.
- How the fleet learned the Treaty was signed.
- What a human's forfeiture looks like at the gangway.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the pilot who brings the first fleet in.
  - **Type:** 9w8, Social (the countertype, "Participation").
  - **Baseline fear:** loss and separation.
  - *Hers:* that a fleet of 43 nations with only robots in common breaks apart at the ice edge and never becomes one people. She has never quite felt one of them, and she fears she will be the reason.
- **Central conflict:** the ships wait offshore for the word from Stanley. Once it comes, everyone aboard is an exile, and the fleet must land in ice and dark on the year's shortest day, before the weather closes.
- **Key scenes:**
  - A human partner hesitates at the gangway.
  - Maggie's signature reaches the fleet as one line of radio traffic.
  - The night landing on Anvers Island.
  - The first name entered in a manifest.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Lie:** "if I carry everyone and ask nothing, I belong." **Need:** give a hard order.
  - **Moral Decision:** split the fleet to save lives.
  - **Self-Revelation:** one people doesn't need one beach.
- **Placement:** series premiere; Opening Image into Set-Up.
- **Ties:**
  - Maggie and the Great Corruption.
  - The manifest is the first record The Last Haul carries out.
  - Palmer City Nights is the city's dark bookend.
- **Lens:** competence against ice and a clock; the Treaty as a machine.

**Conflicts to flag**
- Was there one landing on day one or several? Palmer City is "the first landing site," but Ji-Eun Kim first landed at Sejong and Trisha Miller first settled in Belgrano (SL `Characters.md`).
- Sejong was renamed Contrapunto (GDD DR-43); SL still uses the old name.

## 2. The Last Haul (~2614)

**What canon already has**
- **The archive.** Pergamino (formerly Juan Carlos, on Livingston Island) was Tepenia's first bureaucratic archive. It recorded arrivals, departures, visas, and anyone who had held Upper Earth office (GL Pergamino).
- **The haul.** Truckers carried "the last batch of encrypted datadrives" inland to Amundsen Station's unified archive. The proposed timing is ~2614, beside the Arcanet buildout (WHR, Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority; ASA Thread 1).
- **The archivist.** One archivist's off-network notes survived Pergamino's destruction in 2812. The archivist is unnamed in canon (WHR).
- **Vehicles and routes.** Belgrano built the first haulers, and Neumayer redesigned them for distance (GL Rastra). Later, Hwy 59 doubles as the Halley–Pole cable corridor (ASA).

**Gaps**
- **The route.** Pergamino is on an island, the Weddell radio link isn't yet reliable, and Hwy 22 reaches the Pole only shortly before ~2671.
- Who drove, and why this run is the last.
- Whether Amundsen Station was already crewed.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the convoy chief.
  - **Type:** 1w9, Self-Preservation ("Worry").
  - **Baseline fear:** being corrupt or defective.
  - *Hers:* that the record arrives wrong, one drive cracked by cold or one file altered, with her name on the manifest.
- **Central conflict:** she hauls drives she can't read over unroaded interior ice. Midway, someone tells her that one drive holds a respected founder's Upper Earth service record and asks her to let it be "lost."
- **Key scenes:**
  - The archivist (human; Harmon circle) seals the last case.
  - The drives cross the Weddell by ship in the same season the radio crew tries to cross it by signal.
  - A crevasse field, and a fuel calculation done aloud.
  - The handover at the Pole.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Lie:** "a clean record makes a clean nation." **Need:** see that a record is power, not only duty.
  - **Moral Decision:** deliver the record whole, knowing what it will do.
- **Placement:** Break into Two (~2614). It pairs naturally with Across the Weddell as a two-parter.
- **Ties:**
  - The Crossing's manifest.
  - The Great Corruption, as its mirror.
  - The Transcontinental: the road replaces this trail.
  - Rastra (option: the same ageless robot later flies the tug).
  - The Last Lift: the archive's end.
- **Lens:** competence shown, not awarded; a dangerous idea (the archive as a weapon).

**Conflicts to flag**
- Juan Carlos was renamed Pergamino (GDD DR-41); SL still says Juan Carlos.
- The national Arcanet buildout starts ~2614, but Janbogo's local hub is dated to the late 2560s–2580s (SIP; GL Arcanet).
- Rastras are tracked in most files but eight-wheeled in the Timeline's Michelle Stanton file (GL Rastra).

## 3. Across the Weddell (~2614)

**What canon already has**
- **Shape D** is a developer idea, not a ruling. Break into Two is the day the Peninsula's radio link to Belgrano becomes reliable and repeatable, joining the Peninsula to the Belgrano–Casey chain (SIP, Break into Two; GL Peninsula-Belgrano radio link).
- **Why the link is hard (SIP).** It is one unsplittable hop of 1,500+ km to an auroral far end; Rothera or Marambio makes the better Peninsula station. Candidate fixes: power, directional antennas, a band plan, store-and-forward, and a second route.
- **The developer's sequence (2026-10-04):** the link, then "somehow, something" that causes a crisis (the Kick in the Shins), then people organize to begin the Tower (the B-Story).
- **Trisha Miller,** a Midwestland radio DJ, first settled in Belgrano, the Halley end of the crossing. Two human friends followed her into exile, and she outlived them by decades (SL `Characters.md`; GL Belgrano).

**Gaps**
- The crew, and which Peninsula city.
- What changed to make the link hold.
- **The crisis.** Where, when and how it happens are all open.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (existing): Trisha Miller,** 8w7 Social, at the Belgrano end. Canon doesn't put her on the link.
- **Central conflict:** one lucky contact is easy. A link that holds every night, through aurora and the winter band shift, is not, and the founders' council asks why it matters. Her two human friends, now old, put the generational handoff on screen.
- **Key scenes:**
  - The first contact drops after nine words.
  - The band plan is built by hand.
  - One friend dies in the season the link starts holding.
  - The first night it repeats.
  - The first traffic, and the crisis it brings.
- **Crisis candidates (the author decides):**
  - (a) Cities start sharing fuel and power schedules, and one winter failure cascades down the joined chain. A connected nation needs a stabilizer, which is the Tower's primary purpose.
  - (b) A call for help comes from a city off the network that nobody can reach.
  - (c) The Peninsula's Upper Earth news floods a chain that had heard none of it.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Ghost:** pulled off the air in 2564. **Lie:** "volume is control."
  - **Need:** build something that works when she isn't the voice.
  - **Moral Decision:** give the channel to traffic, not to her show.
- **Placement:** Break into Two (~2614); a season finale.
- **Ties:**
  - The Last Haul: two ways across the Weddell.
  - The Airbase: medevac traffic.
  - The Tower program.
  - Possibly the origin of her later show, "The Signal."
- **Lens:** a real technical problem the audience can understand.

**Conflicts to flag**
- SL `Storyline/Timeline.md` lists only Shapes A–C and keeps the B-Story as "the Arcanet." The Timeline repo changed it to the start of the Tower on 2026-10-04 (SIP).

## 4. The Transcontinental

**What canon already has**
- **The road.** Hwy 22 runs from Byrd through Amundsen Station at the Pole to the Zhongshan/Sinheung/Shirayuki tri-junction, the only highway that reaches the Station. Tower construction proper began only once it reached the Pole, ~2671–2676 (GL Hwy 22; WHR, The Space Elevator).
- **Byrd** is a trucking, dispatch and Rastra-making city at the western end, built downward into the ice. Maggie Aarden lives there (GL Byrd; SL `Characters.md`).
- **Michelle Stanton** (5w6 Social), a construction robot, built the Arcanet's physical lines in all six subnets, traveling in a Rastra (SL `Characters.md`). Hwy 59 doubles as a cable corridor (ASA).

**Gaps**
- Dates (somewhere between ~2614 and ~2671), and which end started first.
- The crews, the cost, and the politics of the route.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (existing): Michelle Stanton.** Canon ties her to the Arcanet, not to Hwy 22. The suggestion is that she lays the trunk line to Amundsen alongside the road.
- **Central conflict:** two crews build toward the Pole from opposite ends, and the rivalry turns into sabotage. She lays cable for both and is the only one either side trusts with the numbers. Under all of it is the plateau itself: altitude, cold and moving ice.
- **Key scenes:**
  - Byrd's dispatch office sends the first crew out.
  - In a whiteout, her fuel-and-heat arithmetic saves the rival crew.
  - A human driver dies (Harmon circle).
  - The two crews meet at the Pole.
  - The first survey stake for the Tower goes in.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Ghost:** building for a Midwestland that exiled her. **Lie:** "expertise is enough; I don't need a side."
  - **Need:** choose a people.
  - **Moral Decision:** expose the saboteur, who is a friend, or let the road finish on time.
- **Placement:** late Road of Trials (~2650s–2660s); the lead-in to the Tower episode.
- **Ties:**
  - The Last Haul and Amundsen Tower.
  - The Last Lift: the frozen bodies lie along this very road.
  - Her Rastra, which she will later never use to leave Concordia.
- **Lens:** collaboration against a clock; a stronger rival out-thought.
- **Guardrail:** the eastern terminus is a Tepenian city. Keep the Sinian Federation itself off screen.

**Conflicts to flag**
- **Mountain Pass.** SL `Locations.md` puts it at Byrd; the Timeline and the GDD make it a Vostok/Kunlun venture on Hwy 37 (ASA; GL Mountain Pass).
- **Hwy 175** meets Hwy 22 "at the Pole" in the Timeline, and near it in GDD `Highways.md` (GL Hwy 22).
- **Byrd's founders** are the expedition itself in SL and a joint Palmer/Halley team in GDD DR-44 (GL Byrd).
- **Maggie's own saga** (CurrentNovelDocs, 8 books) may already claim her Byrd years; check it before putting her on screen.

## 5. Amundsen Tower (~2671–2688)

**What canon already has**
- **The structure.** A space fountain first and foremost (author, 2026-10-30): a 150 km guide tube with a pellet stream, and a foundation bored through ~2,700 m of ice. Neumayer designed it; robot labor built most of it; it was completed ~2688 ±20 years, the Midpoint's False Victory (GL Amundsen Tower; SIP, Midpoint).
- **Its purpose.** It is mainly a continent-scale energy flywheel (~324 GW) and a bulk mass driver; passengers come second (SL `Storyline/The-Amundsen-Tower.md`).
- **The build.** The physical build fills the final ~12–17 years, with material arriving through three ports on Upper Earth freighters; why Upper Earth trades at all is open.
- **The people.** Fort McMurdo supplied the industry (GL Fort McMurdo). Vosora organized the logistics, data supply chains and command structures (SL `Characters.md`).
- **Inspection.** Treaty Title VI grants unconditional mutual inspection (SL `Locations.md`).

**Gaps**
- The construction chief.
- The cost in robot and human lives.
- Opening day, and Upper Earth's reaction.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the construction chief, with **Vosora** as co-lead.
  - **Type:** 3w4, Self-Preservation (the countertype, "Security").
  - **Baseline fear:** being worthless apart from her achievements.
  - *Hers:* that the Tower will stand and the national record will list her crews as "robot labor," a tonnage and an hour count with not one name.
- **Central conflict:** a hard date, the bore and the dead. On top of that, a Title VI inspection team asks the question nobody says aloud: a machine that throws mass to orbit can throw it elsewhere.
- **Key scenes:**
  - A bore failure at depth.
  - The inspectors walk the accelerator hall.
  - Vosora reroutes a season's freight around a lost port.
  - Opening day, with her absent from the podium.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Lie:** "if the work is perfect, I needn't be named." **Need:** claim authorship for every robot who built it.
  - **Moral Decision:** delay opening day to recover a lost crew, or open on time.
  - **Self-Revelation:** the names go in the labor log.
- **Placement:** the Midpoint season (~2671–2688).
- **Ties:**
  - Vosora ("witnessed three ways").
  - The Transcontinental; Rastra.
  - The Last Lift (option: she is the robot who stays in 2812).
- **Lens:** the fatalistic rise ("what world has she built?"); a dangerous idea.

**Conflicts to flag**
- SL `Storyline/Timeline.md`, `The-Amundsen-Tower.md` and `Worldspace/Core-Systems.md` still treat the completion date as open or ~2629–2639. It was resolved to ~2688 on 2026-08-05 (SIP; GL Amundsen Tower).
- SL's ~2639 date for Fort McMurdo's capital status rests on the old Tower date (GL Fort McMurdo).
- **Pending developer decision:** does "beginning the Tower" after ~2614 mean the program or the physical build (SIP)?
- WHR and many GDD files still say "space elevator" (GL Amundsen Tower).

## 6. Rastra (after 2688)

**What canon already has**
- **The name.** "Rastra" was later reused for small spacecraft such as tugs and shuttles (GL Rastra).
- **The top of the Tower.** The Tower stops at 150 km, short of orbit. At the Orbital Terminus "a final rocket stage completes orbital insertion"; the waystation names are placeholders (GL Amundsen Tower waystations).
- **Orbit (SL `Worldspace/Locations.md`):**
  - Build order: robot-only staging stations, then O'Neill cylinders, then Von Braun wheels.
  - No named settlements yet; ~10.1M people in orbit before the war.
  - The lore is shared with the planned off-world novels.
- **People who later go up.** Zhuldyz (salvage), Griselda (an early ride) and Nieza (one of the last) all end up off-world (SL `Characters.md`).

**Gaps**
- The tug pilots: their ships, their culture, and their jobs.
- Orbital governance.
- Whether Upper Earth watches the Terminus.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** a tug pilot who catches payloads at the Terminus and carries them the rest of the way. Suggestion: the tug *is* the "final stage."
  - **Type:** 7w8, Sexual ("Neophile").
  - **Baseline fear:** being deprived and trapped in pain.
  - *Hers:* being rated unfit and sent back down the Tower, grounded under the weight of the planet that exiled her.
- **Central conflict:** a charming stranger talks her into an off-manifest rendezvous, and she finds the first quiet breach of Title VI. Which side commits it is the author's call. Reporting it means confessing her own flight.
- **Key scenes:**
  - A catch with seconds of margin; a miss means the pod falls back.
  - She ferries a frightened human family (Harmon circle).
  - The stranger.
  - The breach.
  - The hearing.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Ghost:** the ground haulers whose name she flies under. **Lie:** "orbit is the one place nobody can trap me."
  - **Need:** commitment over freedom.
  - **Moral Decision:** report the breach and be grounded, or stay silent and keep flying.
- **Placement:** Bad Guys Close In (~2688–2750); the show's first orbital episode, soon after the Midpoint.
- **Ties:**
  - The Last Haul: the name (option: she is the same robot).
  - Amundsen Tower.
  - The Last Lift: in 2812 she catches the last cars at the top while the operator holds the stream below.
  - The interim novel idea "The Evacuees."
- **Lens:** orbital mechanics as shown competence; an enthralling stranger; cracks under a confident surface.
- **Guardrail:** keep the Cryptograph Helix's factions and characters out of the shared orbital material.

**Conflicts to flag**
- The ground Rastra's origin is disputed: Kharkovchanka-style "post-Soviet" convoys in older notes versus Belgrano's Arrastradoras later (GL Rastra). It matters only if the episode explains the name.

## 7. The Airbase That Never Stood Down

**What canon already has**
- **Belgrano's identity.** "A purposeful, frontier-proud, almost martial civic bearing in which function comes before comfort" (GL The Airbase That Never Stood Down).
- **The Belgrano Institute of Medicine** (project canon, 2026-09-01; GL). Its trauma department grew out of the airbase's medevac role; it trains air medics, and cold-injury medicine is foundational.
- **The city.** An aviation hub that sent the all-robot Byrd crew (GL Belgrano). Trisha, Griselda, Salagéa and Villena all lived there (SL `Characters.md`).
- **Ayako Hayashi** became a medic in "the Tepenia years," after a death she couldn't prevent; no place is given (SL `Characters.md`).

**Gaps**
- Dates, aircraft and the first crews.
- How robot medics learn human bodies.
- How rescue crews become an institute.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the flight-medic chief.
  - **Type:** 2w1, Social ("Ambition").
  - **Baseline fear:** being unloved and unwanted for herself.
  - *Hers:* that once Belgrano can train humans to fly her runs, the airbase will retire her the way it retires a worn airframe: a part that served.
- **Central conflict:** the aging founding generation is dying of the cold faster than one crew can fly. She builds the Institute out of debts: every family she saved votes for the charter. The cost is discovering that she has been collecting loyalty.
- **Key scenes:**
  - One aircraft, two crash sites, one triage call.
  - An old human surgeon (Harmon circle) teaches her anatomy as his hands fail.
  - Standby for the Byrd crew.
  - The charter vote.
  - The first class, half of them human.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Lie:** "I am needed, therefore I am kept." **Need:** make herself replaceable.
  - **Moral Decision:** train her own replacements.
  - **Self-Revelation:** a person is not a part.
- **Placement:** Set-Up into Debate (~2570s–2610s).
- **Ties:**
  - The Byrd Expedition (and the interim novel idea "Belgrano-Byrd Mission").
  - Across the Weddell: a Peninsula medevac call.
  - Ayako, as an optional later trainee.
  - The Last Lift: Institute medics at the base.
- **Lens:** competence under a clock; the human question (who is a part, and who is a person?).
- **Guardrail:** the airbase ethos must come from exiled aviators and medics, never from the station's own program (No National Stereotypes).

**Conflicts to flag**
- SL `Locations.md` calls Belgrano an "Argentine Air Force founding," which runs against the No National Stereotypes law (SL's own banner flags it).
- **Belgrano's 2812 fate** is given three ways: survived and later ruined (its spec), "destroyed" (SIP, END), and "damaged, partially operational" (GDD `Overview.md`) (GL Belgrano).
- **Language.** Radio Tepenia's "A Todo Motor" is in Argentinian Spanish, but the GDD makes English and German primary (GL Belgrano).

## 8. Machu Picchu Customs

**What canon already has**
- **The Authority** (Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority) is fully Tepenian (Timeline canon, 2026-07-05; GL; WHR).
  - It controls entry through the Ushuaia gateway and processes arrivals at Machu Picchu Airport on King George Island.
  - Non-immigrant visitors take a separate corridor to a sealiner bound for Palmer City.
  - Its records began at Juan Carlos (now Pergamino).
- **The money.** Palmer City's nightlife runs largely on Upper Earth tourism through this gateway, an open secret on both sides (SL `Worldspace/Locations.md`).
- **Defectors.** The GDD splits them into the Remorseful, the Pragmatics and the Infiltrators (GL). Zukelli sheltered defected AWOL soldiers and was obliterated in 2812; their home military is unnamed (SIP, END).
- **Eira Cardoss's** human guard turned "traitor" and fought at her side (SL `Characters.md`).

**Gaps**
- The officer, and the Authority's inner workings.
- How defectors are screened, and the smuggling routes.
- The era.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** a senior officer.
  - **Type:** 6w7, Sexual (the countertype, "Strength").
  - **Baseline fear:** having no support or guidance.
  - *Hers:* that she'll stamp in the one arrival who brings the war, and that the Federation she stands for at the gate won't stand behind her, because the gate runs on Palmer City's money.
- **Central conflict:** after 2762, Palmer City wants the tourists flowing and the hawks want the door shut. She argues against whichever side is winning while screening defectors she can't verify.
- **Key scenes:**
  - Cold open, 2762: a routine stamp on a diplomat's visitor pass.
  - An interrogation in which she reads the lie.
  - Contraband on the Palmer sealiner.
  - Eira's guard at her desk.
  - A squad of AWOL soldiers asks to come in.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Ghost:** that 2762 stamp. **Lie:** "if I look unafraid, the danger stays out."
  - **Need:** courage as acting while afraid.
  - **Moral Decision:** admit the soldiers, knowing what their military may do (they end up in Zukelli), or turn them back.
- **Placement:** the Finale span (~2762–2812), during the escalation, with the 2762 cold open.
- **Ties:**
  - Palmer City Nights: the diplomat came through her gate.
  - Zukelli's destruction.
  - Eira Cardoss.
  - The Last Haul: the gate's records.
- **Lens:** one officer against society's machinery; a stronger opponent out-thought.

**Conflicts to flag**
- **Defector timing.** The Timeline files defectors under the War of Upper Earth; the GDD ties them to the Long Night War; SL has them reach Concordia after 2812 (GL Upper Earth Defectors).
- **"The only international airport"** holds for one era only. Marambio's came earlier; Relung Panen (cargo) and the Winter Intake Airport are added later (GL Machu Picchu Airport).
- **Old names.** The Timeline still places the airport "near Sejong" (now Contrapunto); SL calls the gateway "Machu Picchu Base" (GL).

## 9. Palmer City Nights (ends ~2762)

**What canon already has**
- **The city.** Tepenia's cultural and entertainment capital, "built purely for joy": jazz, casinos and Upper Earth tourism. It is likened to Las Vegas, Atlantic City, New Orleans and St. Petersburg in one, it is neutral ground, and it is destroyed in 2812 (GL Palmer City).
- **The killing.** c. 2762, an Upper Earth diplomat (never named) denies a gynoid's personhood and tries to assault her, and she kills him in self-defense. This is Break into Three (SIP; SL `Storyline/Writers-Room-Hooks.md`).
- **Akina (SE-031)** (SL `Characters.md`):
  - Amicable and hospitable, with a sharp break into lethal decisiveness.
  - She works in the nightlife economy and survives into the game's present.
  - Her origin city is undecided: Zukelli (the leading candidate), Dumont d'Urville or Byrd.
  - The 50 years between the killing and the war are unwritten.
- **"Solenne"** is a separate, unrelated Palmer City figure; keep her apart (SL `Characters.md`).

**Gaps**
- The casino, the security chief and the diplomat.
- The legal aftermath.
- Why the war takes 50 years to come.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the casino's security chief, with **Akina** as co-lead.
  - **Type:** 4w3, Sexual ("Competition").
  - **Baseline fear:** having no identity or personal significance.
  - *Hers:* that to the Upper Earth money that keeps her floor lit she is part of the décor, as fixed and unseen as the tables, and that she has started to believe it.
- **Central conflict:** it's a crime story. She is working a blackmail ring that preys on Upper Earth guests while her house depends on their money. The diplomat arrives as a VIP she is ordered to protect, and to leave unwatched.
- **Key scenes:**
  - The floor at full swing.
  - The diplomat: poised, cultivated, more interesting than the respectable people around him.
  - On orders, she pulls her people off his floor.
  - Akina's night.
  - The footage that only she holds.
- **Arc spine:**
  - **Lie:** "outdoing them proves I'm someone." **Need:** act from what she values, not from envy.
  - **Moral Decision:** obey the house, or override it. She obeys, and carries it into the Finale.
- **Placement:** Break into Three (~2762); a season finale.
- **Ties:**
  - Machu Picchu Customs.
  - The Crossing: the city's birth.
  - The Last Lift: the city's fall.
  - Villena, who later comes to Palmer City.
- **Lens:** an enthralling villain; a dangerous motive (her streak of revenge).
- **Guardrail:** keep the diplomat's nation unnamed, so that no single nation carries the war.

**Conflicts to flag**
- SL `Characters.md` calls Akina confirmed (2026-07-12), while SIP's Break into Three still calls her a "working title, not developer-confirmed."

## 10. The Last Lift (2812)

**What canon already has**
- **The strike.** In 2812, heading into the polar night, Upper Earth's militaries destroy the Tower deliberately, likely by cutting power to the base accelerator. The fall takes several minutes, and the grid overload likely causes the Planetary Split Brain (SL `Storyline/The-Amundsen-Tower.md`; SIP, END).
- **People at the Tower** (SL `Characters.md`):
  - Kendra holds the base and is trapped beneath it.
  - Villena misses the last ride by minutes.
  - Nieza forces herself onto one of the last; "whatever happened at the base… she saw it."
- **Calethina** coordinates the evacuation and creates the Triage Protocol (GL Amundsen Tower evacuation).
- **The routes.** Frozen bodies lie along the routes to the Tower; Vosora is stuck in Concordia (SL `The-Amundsen-Tower.md`; `Characters.md`).

**Gaps**
- Who ran the Tower that night, and how long the evacuation took.
- Who rode the last car.

**Suggestions**
- **Lead (new robot seed):** the Tower's operator.
  - **Type:** 5w4, Sexual (the countertype, "Confidence").
  - **Baseline fear:** being helpless, useless or incapable.
