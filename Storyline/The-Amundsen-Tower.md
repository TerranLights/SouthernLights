# The Amundsen Tower — Southern Lights' Season-Centerpiece Infrastructure

Source: InnerTepeniaGDD `Theoretical-Calculations/Amundsen_Tower_Space_Fountain_Design.md` (917 lines) plus `Design_Efficiency_Comparison.md`, `Orbital_Infrastructure_Mass_Budget.md`, `Von_Braun_Wheel_Mass_Budget.md`, and `Worldspace/World_History_Reference.md`. See also [`Timeline.md`](Timeline.md) for the unresolved completion-date tension (~2629–2639 established vs. ~2688 tentative Midpoint placement — **not yet settled even in the source's own canon**; don't lock Southern Lights to either date until that's resolved, or decide independently and flag the divergence).

## What it actually was

Named after Amundsen Station (the South Pole site). **Not a classic space elevator** — a real elevator's tension-cable/counterweight mechanism depends on the structure being fixed to a rotating point with meaningful surface velocity, which doesn't exist at the pole (zero rotational velocity on the axis). Physically impossible to build a standard elevator there.

Instead it's a **space fountain**: a real (never-built-in-reality) proposed megastructure. A continuous stream of pellets is electromagnetically launched upward at 4 km/s, decelerated and looped back down magnetically, with magnetic bearing stations along a 150km guide tube nudging the pellet stream and being nudged back by Newton's third law — the same principle as a jet of water holding up a floating ball. This works at *any* latitude, including the pole, which is exactly why it's the setting's chosen solution.

Height: 150km — clears the Kármán line, short of full LEO. Foundation bores through ~2,700m of real Antarctic ice to bedrock (matching the real Amundsen-Scott Station's actual ice depth).

## What it connected

Tepenia to low-earth orbit — **not** to Upper Earth (non-Antarctic Earth). Marks the start of Tepenian space colonization: low-earth orbit first, then Mars.

## Who built it, and when

Built largely by robot labor under extreme conditions — the Tepenian Federation's own effort, both robot and human citizens. Immense source of national pride: a civilizational achievement built by the people who had been exiled there, proving themselves on their own terms.

**Confirmed phased build sequence** (order is locked canon; exact dates are the open tension noted above): (1) cities founded on existing station infrastructure, (2) subnet-internal highway networks, (3) the Arcanet extended subnet by subnet, (4) Hwy 22 (the Transcontinental Highway) built from Byrd through the South Pole to the Zhongshan coast. **Only once Hwy 22 reached the Pole did Tower construction proper begin** — likely in the final ~12–17 years of the roughly 65–75-year post-Treaty window. Material logistics were never the real bottleneck (shipping the ~3.225 million tonnes of material only took ~4–10 years via a three-port network: Janbogo year-round, Halley and Byrd/Fort McMurdo seasonal) — most of the build window went into the preceding decades of nation-building and developing the underlying technology, not the physical assembly itself.

**Real-world shipping flavor already established:** South African freighters resupply the Halley subnet (mirroring real SANAP/*SA Agulhas II* voyages); New Zealand freighters run the Ross Sea/Janbogo/Byrd routes out of Christchurch; Australian freighters run the Mirny/Mawson coast out of Hobart. **Explicitly flagged as unresolved even in source canon:** why Upper Earth nations would trade with an exiled robot nation at all, given the anti-robot sentiment that caused the exile in the first place — open material for Southern Lights (economic pragmatism vs. the professed politics of the people doing the trading).

## What it was actually for (reframed 2026-07-05 in the source — recent, important)

**Passenger transport was never the primary purpose.** Working the throughput math backward, the design already moves the entire established off-world population ~6.4 times over across its ~120-year operational life at realistic capacity — far more capacity than passenger transport alone would justify. The Tower's real reasons for existing:

1. **Continent-scale energy regulation** — the pellet stream acts as a giant flywheel, drawing ~324 GW net (a meaningful fraction of current real-world global electricity generation), continuously stabilizing Tepenia's entire power grid.
2. **Bulk material mass-driving** — its native function as a mass driver, moving cargo/ore/manufactured goods to and from orbit. Passengers ride separate maglev cars alongside the pellet stream, mechanically independent of it.

Passenger transport was real, but a secondary application riding on infrastructure that existed for other reasons. This reframing matters a lot for how big a loss its destruction represents — see below.

## Destruction (2812, the Long Night War)

Deliberately destroyed by Upper Earth's military — not incidental battle damage, but a targeted strike meant to sever Tepenia's off-world lifeline and annihilate the single thing that most represented what the exiled population had built for itself. Likely method: cutting power to the base accelerator (far more plausible than physically demolishing a 150km structure).

**Two-axis catastrophe** (established 2026-07-05 in the source): because the Tower was the grid's stabilizing flywheel, its destruction plausibly caused a simultaneous continent-wide electrical "load rejection" overload — independent of, and in addition to, the physical collapse. This is also the likely direct mechanism behind the **Planetary Split Brain** (the Arcanet's inter-subnet backbone depended on the same Tower-regulated grid; when it overloaded, the inter-subnet links were fried/severed while each subnet's local network survived intact — explaining why the resulting information fracture aligns exactly with subnet boundaries).

**Physical debris:** the Tower is Earth-anchored, not orbiting, so cutting power just makes it fall — none of the wreckage reaches orbit or scatters far. ~93% of debris (~228,875 t) reaches the ground, forming a scrap mountain 27–109m tall depending on concentration, spreading only 2–10km from the base (Coriolis deflection is exactly zero at the pole, so wind is the only lateral-spread mechanism). **Confirmed: the scrap mountain never reaches Concordia** (1,660km away) — this is a localized catastrophe, not continent-spanning. Matters for how Southern Lights stages it visually: a towering, localized ruin at the South Pole, not debris rain across the show's whole geography.

## Unbuilt dramatic material flagged directly in the source

From the Tower's own design doc (TL;DR item 4): the single most affecting unwritten beat connected to its fall is **the journey to the Tower, not the Tower's throughput** — "frozen bodies (human and robot) found along the routes various subnets' populations took while trying to reach Amundsen Tower during the Long Night War, many not making it in time." Explicitly logged in the source as unbuilt, high-value material. A strong candidate for a Southern Lights season-finale image.

## A last-stand character already attached to the Tower's fall

**Kendra Heinrich** (DLC1 "Echoes of Amundsen," set at the ruins ~10–15 years post-war) — an M10/A10/N10/E10 robot, described as a "war goddess," who single-handedly held off an Upper Earth assault force during the evacuation, then retreated inside the station and locked herself in once too damaged to keep fighting in the open. The source's confirmed answer to "what defeated her": not a single stronger enemy, but a war of attrition — extreme cold, structural collapse of the debris field, residual live-electrical hazards in the severed accelerator wreckage, and enemies preventing her recovery, over years, alone, with no resupply. She's been stranded, waiting, for over a decade by the time DLC1's present-day player reaches her — "too proud" to call for help even if she still could.

If Southern Lights wants a Long-Night-War-era action set piece with a tragic, ongoing coda that the game's own present-day story picks back up, this character is already fully designed.
