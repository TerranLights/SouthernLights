For the Falkland Treaty's real-world treaty models and the Maggie Aarden/Great Corruption threads, see [`../Storyline/The-Falkland-Treaty.md`](../Storyline/The-Falkland-Treaty.md) — this doc covers the Treaty's *internal legal structure* only, to avoid duplicating that material.

## The Falkland Treaty's internal structure (2564-06-21)

**[SIP 2564 — first full draft only, dated 2026-07-08, "not locked canon"]** Deliberately written as a victor's settlement imposed on a defeated, exiled population, genre-modeled on the Treaty of New Echota (1835) — a preamble that never states extermination was the alternative, instead framing exile as something "sought and been granted." Preamble + seven Titles:

- **Title I — Founding the Federation of Tepenia.** Sovereign in Antarctica; doesn't specify internal government form (deferred to an unwritten Tepenian Constitution).
- **Title II — Territorial & Population Provisions.** Sovereignty Freeze (Art. II.1, modeled on the real Antarctic Treaty Art. IV); the Exile Provision (Art. II.2) — blanket, no individual finding of wrongdoing required "nor shall the absence of any such finding be grounds for exception"; population allocated to existing research stations with a path to self-government (a UN Trusteeship System borrow).
- **Title III — Regional Governance.** Treaty-level basis for Tepenia's six Arcanet subnets as semi-autonomous regional bodies (UN Charter Ch. VIII borrow).
- **Title IV — Rights, Restrictions & Peaceful Use.** Art. IV.1 strips the 2318 Jeju-do robot-personhood ruling on Upper Earth soil, no effect inside Tepenia. Art. IV.2, humans who elect exile are deemed to have "willingly and permanently relinquished" personhood. Art. IV.3, peaceful-use clause with named exceptions — a deliberate, Locarno-style loophole.
- **Title V — Scientific/Economic/Energy Cooperation.** Nuclear prohibition (retroactively explains Tepenia's reliance on Amundsen Tower grid-regulation instead); a 10-year material-assistance schedule from Upper Earth, explicitly "not a full accounting for what has been lost."
- **Title VI — Inspection & Transparency.** Real, unconditional mutual inspection rights — written with force specifically so its eventual secret violation reads as the treaty's actual failure point.
- **Title VII — Amendment, Review & Withdrawal.** Art. VII.2, a 30-year scheduled review conference. **Art. VII.3 — the single most dangerous clause, by design:** either party may unilaterally withdraw on its own "vital interests" judgment, three months' notice, no committee approval needed. Flagged as the mechanical seed candidate for the Long Night War — not an external villain, just a founding nation invoking a clause the treaty always guaranteed it.

**Signatories:** the near-universal United Assembly of Nations. Robot/human-ally signatory: Maggie Aarden.

## The Federation's cities — founding & fate

All ~35 cities map 1:1 onto real Antarctic research stations, organized into six Arcanet subnets: **Palmer** (Antarctic Peninsula), **Halley** (Weddell Sea coast), **Mawson** (East Antarctic Plateau), **Mirny** (East Antarctic coast/Larsemann Hills), **Janbogo** (Ross Sea), **Byrd** (single city, Pacific). Amundsen Station sits outside any subnet as neutral inter-subnet relay. **All entries [SIP 2564–2812] for founding; fate marks the 2812 transition into POST-WAR.**

| City | 2812 fate | Founding story |
|---|---|---|
| **Palmer City** (Anvers Island) | destroyed | First Tepenian city, founded the exact day of the Treaty. "The Place Everyone Passed Through" — all 43 possible founding nations represented. Cultural (never governmental) capital; nightlife substantially funded by Upper Earth tourist revenue via the Machu Picchu customs gateway — an open secret both sides. **The Long Night War's inciting killing (c. 2762) happens here.** |
| **Fort McMurdo** (Ross Island) | damaged, partially operational | Informal de facto capital from early on; formally designated only later (~2639, once Amundsen Tower construction made its industrial dependency undeniable). Capital title does NOT survive the war and passes to no successor — Tepenia is deliberately, permanently capital-less afterward. |
| **Byrd** | survives — one of only three cities (with Concordia and Janbogo) still functioning "as a city" post-war | Founded by an all-robot expedition — see below. Also site of "Mountain Pass," a historical robot-fabrication facility that ran on Amundsen Tower's residual grid power, ending permanently when the Tower fell. |
| **Amundsen Station** (South Pole) | destroyed | Not residential — inter-subnet Arcanet relay + Tower operations facility, ~81% robot. Politically neutral ground for inter-subnet disputes while it stood. Its destruction causes the Planetary Split Brain and holds the last synchronized pre-split archive of Tepenian history. |

**Byrd's founding — the strongest standalone founding-era episode in the dataset.** Byrd Station had been buried and untraceable since 2005. Belgrano, Palmer City, and Rothera triangulated its location from salvaged records; an all-robot expedition crew — chosen specifically because the crossing was too dangerous to risk human lives — drove a purpose-built hauler across unmapped interior terrain, found only snowfield, discovered buried tunnels, and built the city downward first, then outward, then up.

**Population:** Census I (pre-orbital, peak surface population) 32,026,600 total (15.6M human / 16.4M robot) across 37 locations. Census II (orbital era, immediately pre-war) — same total, split 21.9M surface / 10.1M orbit (relocated, not born/died). Combined war losses: ~2,874,000 (~9%), flagged as a likely undercount. Refugee flows into Concordia are extensively mapped city-by-city to district — two documented trauma-driven overrides worth knowing: Scorpio (hosting the Archive of Final Confessions) becomes the top-2 refugee destination for all four destroyed/ruined cities for grief-processing reasons, and Gemini draws Zukelli refugees for data-archaeology/testimony-recovery reasons — despite neither matching those cities' actual pre-war culture.

## Concordia & the thirteen districts

Concordia Station is a real French-Italian joint research facility, Dome C, East Antarctic Plateau (75°06'S, 123°21'E, ~3,233m elevation, mean annual −52.7°C, ~82 days total polar night, ~85 days midnight sun). Twelve districts are zodiac-named purely as an out-of-fiction developer scheme — **no one in-universe uses or recognizes the zodiac naming.** The 13th, unzoned district is the Hub (Axis Mundi), the neutral central crossroads.

**Important for the show:** most of what reads as Long Night War trauma in the district material is actually 250-year-old Second Interwar Period culture the war only stress-tested. Four districts (Libra, Gemini, Virgo, the Hub) are specifically confirmed to carry this misattribution — don't default to "the war caused this" when writing district texture for the show's own timeframe.

**The central reframe:** all thirteen districts share one identical underlying goal — for Concordia to become "a true city... not a collection of buildings" — differing only on method:

| District | Theory of how a city becomes real |
|---|---|
| Cancer | By taking care of its most vulnerable, unconditionally. |
| Taurus | Through accumulated, unbroken domestic continuity. |
| Leo | Through shared culture and undeniable collective experience. |
| Scorpio | By confronting what it actually is, not managing it from a distance. |
| Aries | Through relentless, uncomplaining production. |
| Capricorn | Through visible, measurable achievement. |
| Aquarius | By inventing itself anew, from first principles. |
| Libra | Through fair, stable process. |
| Gemini | Through shared knowledge and connection. |
| Pisces | By having room for everyone the other theories fail. |
| Sagittarius | By pushing outward — proof it hasn't stopped growing. |
| Virgo | From the ground up, literally — infrastructure that works. |
| The Hub | By having neutral ground where all the other theories can actually meet. |

**Per-district detail, all founded late 2560s–2590s unless noted, all [SIP] for their founding-era material:**

- **Cancer** ("The Sanctuary"): sanctuary/integration zone for the *original* exile trauma. SIP pressure: chronic power disputes with Aries endangering incubators/medical equipment. Care-debt parallel economy, curving no-straight-lines architecture, the "Mother's Circuit" caregiving-robot network drifting toward surveillance.
- **Taurus** ("The Hearth"): first fully planned residential sector for bonded human-robot pairs. SIP pressure: the Insulation Schism — materials repeatedly diverted to Capricorn during shortages, producing a permanent "Cold Side"/"Warm Side" stratification.
- **Leo** ("The Golden Ring"): morale district built to combat exile's psychological toll. Named SIP events: **the Star War** (mid-2740s, ~15-year internal civil war between two rival performance houses, nominally aesthetic, functionally a power fight — the losing house's descendants remain, still bitter) and **the Dimming** (2789–90, Aries cuts Leo's power for six months during an energy crisis; hundreds of performers leave; remembered as sacred betrayal, still governs district power-priority politics generations later).
- **Scorpio** ("The Veil"): Cancer's sibling district — same founding window and wound, opposite method (confrontation not shelter).
- **Aries** ("The Core"): prioritized immediately post-Treaty as the geothermal/nuclear power core. Suppression-through-labor as its wound response.
- **Capricorn** ("The Yards"): heavy manufacturing/infrastructure hub, built the domes that made other districts possible. **The Merit Board Audit** (c. 2761, tentative — the developer isn't sure they'll keep it) — an independent review finding the merit-rating system systematically undervalued robot workers vs. humans for decades; leadership disputes/buries the findings.
- **Aquarius** ("The Labs"): dedicated R&D sector, tries to engineer past the exile wound rather than process it. Three named utopian attempts, all undone by the same unacknowledged dependency on Aries/Virgo/Capricorn: **the First Collective** (~2590s, 18 months) and **the Open Frame Project** (~2740s, 7 years, contamination event) both fall inside the show's window.
- **Libra** ("The Government District"): central governing/mediation hub from chaotic early exile. **The Suspended Compact** — a 230-year-old, still-buried founding-era constitutional document promising democratic district representation, suspended under wartime emergency powers that, by design, never actually end.
- **Gemini** ("Janbogo Subnet Nexus"): central Arcanet routing/data nexus from early exile. **The Great Corruption** — see [`../Storyline/The-Falkland-Treaty.md`](../Storyline/The-Falkland-Treaty.md).
- **Pisces** ("The Markets"): began as an unofficial overflow zone. **The Flood** (c. 2771) — an experimental neural-interface mod malfunctions, causing involuntary mass memory-sharing; likely the empirical origin of the district's later Syncretic Religion.
- **Sagittarius** ("The Frostlands"): launch point for surface expeditions. **The Third Expansion Collapse** (a self-inflicted trauma — a dome-extension project fails, sixty-three die) and **the Siligel Rush and Bust** (from c. 2775 — a major precursor-material quarry cluster creates a boom; Sagittarius briefly holds real political power; quarries deplete faster than expected; boom infrastructure remains, political power doesn't; lasting bitterness toward central-district economic promises).
- **Virgo** ("The Undergrid"): built the redundant heating loops, waste recyclers, and structural reinforcement that made surface habitation possible, present from day one — 250 years before the war (a corrected misattribution; not war-created). **The Deep Level** — unexplained pre-Concordia chambers below the known Undergrid, origin genuinely unresolved (pre-Falkland infrastructure? something older?). Home to the Virgo Deep Level Keeper (see [`Characters.md`](Characters.md)).
- **The Hub (Axis Mundi)**: the central transit/administrative node from the beginning; Calethina's activation lab was established here under the same neutral-jurisdiction logic, one of the oldest, most deliberately designed civic institutions in the city.

**Concordia's Second Interwar identity as a whole:** "A City Built on the Way Up" — one of several major Federation inland cities growing alongside a Federation building its greatest infrastructure, culminating in the Amundsen Tower ("the proudest moment the Federation had yet produced"). SIP-era industry split: scientific research 25%, industrial/manufacturing 25%, governance 15%, entertainment/culture 15%, agriculture 10%, other 10%. **Concordia does not become "the last city" until 2812, the show's final year — for nearly its whole runtime, the show should depict Concordia as an ordinary, ambitious, growing inland city, not yet the last refuge it becomes in the game's era.**

## Upper Earth & orbital infrastructure

Upper Earth is the collective term for the rest of Earth's nations outside Antarctica — victor of the War of Upper Earth, imposer of the Falkland Treaty, and a hypocritical trade partner: officially disdainful of Tepenia, but privately floods Palmer City's nightlife with tourist revenue via the Machu Picchu customs gateway. Politically fragmented post-2083 into dozens of named successor states under the near-universal United Assembly of Nations.

**Orbital infrastructure is deliberately shared source-of-truth material** — established explicitly across three separate projects: *Inner Tepenia* itself, a planned Mars/Jupiter/Saturn-colonization novel series, and, **named directly in the source**, "a planned TV series spanning the entire Second Interwar Period, arrival on Antarctic shores through the Concordia refugee migration" — this show. Southern Lights and the novel series are meant to draw on the same orbital lore rather than duplicate it. Three structure types, in build order: robot-only staging stations first, then **O'Neill Cylinders** (primary long-term residence), then **Von Braun Wheels** (mobile/expansion-crew infrastructure). Census II puts the orbital population at 10,104,964 (5.1M human / 5.0M robot) — ~31.6% of Tepenia's total. No named individual orbital settlements exist yet — only structure types, open territory. By the game's present, the off-world diaspora is confirmed thriving (Phobos/Deimos infrastructure, active Mars colonization, presence extending toward Venus) — vastly outgrown Tepenia, unaware their old home doesn't know it.
