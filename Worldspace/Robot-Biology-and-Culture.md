# Robot Biology & Culture — Inherited from InnerTepeniaGDD

Source: `Worldspace/Robot_Biology_and_Culture/`, cross-referenced with `Characters/Dolls/Doll_Visual_Design_Canon.md`. Compiled 2026-07-10.

**The single most load-bearing section for the show's premise: how an ageless robot POV cast could plausibly function, on-screen, across 248 years.**

## No aging, no breathing

Robots do not age biologically — components wear and need maintenance/replacement, but this is mechanical wear, categorically distinct from biological aging. No respiratory system, no oxygen requirement; can operate in hard vacuum (including on spacecraft hulls) with no protective gear. Physical form is patterned conceptually on the film *Ex Machina* — no traditional hard-shelled frame/body, for either the fabrication chamber or the robot brain itself.

## The visual tell

Otherwise fully indistinguishable from humans — synthetic skin, hair, human proportions, no visible mechanical parts under normal conditions. The one universal marker: **a soft internal iris glow (iris only, not sclera) that shifts color/saturation/luminescence involuntarily with emotional state** — a built-in nonverbal-acting device. Reading it is a learned social skill — common knowledge in mixed communities like Concordia but **not** known to isolated populations (Upper Earth defectors, frontier settlers) — a ready-made way to mark a character's background/exposure to Dolls on sight. Build types differ by function (heavy-duty maintenance models like Griselda Chávez are physically robust), but internal differences (frame fortification, gel-brain architecture, battery capacity) are invisible externally. Damage/repair shows like human injury — scarring, skin-graft lines, tone mismatches, cold/chemical-exposure pigmentation changes — never exposed frame or metalwork.

## Aging and death

The core Physiology doc is silent on this — flagged as an open gap in the source itself. Per the Visual Design Canon: Dolls live far longer than humans but do die. External aging shows as skin texture/quality change, not human-style elderly deterioration. Internal death vectors: **gel brain degradation** (memory/processing/personality coherence loss), **battery ("heart") burnout** (typically terminal), and **central personality module corruption** (the body survives, the self doesn't — "its own category of horror"). Near-death shows via unstable/dimming iris luminescence and erratic emotional iris-shifts.

## Manufacturing eras are visually dateable

**En.Ki.Du** (built 2083 at U.R.U.K.) is the only Doll ever built with an overtly robotic exterior — every Doll built after him looks fully human. Circa-2500s-built Dolls have skin with less dynamic environmental responsiveness than modern skin. Early-era Dolls (through the late 2500s) tend toward more complex, layered iris patterns, since early manufacturing leaned on irises as primary sensory arrays. **Usable directly as a visual shorthand for a character's build-era across the show's 248-year span** — a robot introduced in 2564 can visibly read as "older tech" by 2812 without a single line of dialogue.

## Siligel & coolant

**Siligel** (synthesized from mined precursors, manufactured centrally in Capricorn) is robot food — lethal to humans, an entirely separate supply chain from human food, doubling the city's systemic vulnerability surface. **Coolant** is robot drink, managing internal heat, varying in formulation/quality like human beverages; "robot coffee" is a stimulant-formulated coolant. **Glitch-coolant** is robot alcohol — coolant mixed with electrolytic compounds producing a deliberate "glitching" effect analogous to human drunkenness, drunk recreationally after work shifts — a genuine Tepenia-wide cultural institution varying by city character (wide/refined/milder in cosmopolitan cities like Janbogo, Zhongshan, Shirayuki; limited-but-strong in working-class cities like Byrd, Rothera; working-class-strong-but-high-quality in freight-hub Troll).

## Smoking is canon

Robots have internal atmospheric-management systems (thermal regulation, sensory-input processing) that produce real thermal sensation, chemical interaction with internal sensors, and a genuine internal-state shift when drawing vapor through them — not identical to human dopamine response but real in its own terms. The "hands-and-fire" theory: human smoking unifies the hand (manual dexterity) and fire (harnessed energy); for a robot, the hand/manipulator gesture is identical in form, but "fire" moves inward — a robot doesn't harness an external flame, it *is* an energy system, so its own internal energy processing *is* the fire. Whether a given robot subjectively experiences pleasure from it is deliberately left ambiguous by design — tied to the setting's central, unanswerable question about robot consciousness. Confirmed smokers: Naizelle d'Edjordoś, Zhuldyz Serikbolatkyzy.

## The Cradle — how robots are made

Robots are physically created via fabrication-synthesis chambers, manufactured in only a handful of Tepenian cities and shipped nationwide — a robot can be "born" anywhere regardless of whether that city itself manufactures chambers. Confirmed active manufacturing sites: an unnamed Korean city and Byrd. Confirmed historical (no longer active) sites: **Mountain Pass** (joint Vostok/Kunlun venture, ran on Amundsen Tower's residual overflow power — permanently ended when the Tower fell in 2812) and **Denison** (lost when the city was destroyed). Chambers are durable, not consumable — every surviving city historically received a shipped chamber, so robots can still be "born" anywhere with zero new production, as long as existing chambers are maintained.

**Cultural distinction, worth building the show's whole ethos around:** on Upper Earth, humans commission robots to their own specifications — a consumer relationship. In Tepenia this inverts: **robots build each other according to their own interests**, an act of robot self-determination, not service provision. Construction takes real time, not instant.

## Society baseline

Human-robot relations are "egalitarian, skewed in favor of robots" — roughly equal labor division with robots tending to hold slightly better positions, a quiet reflection that Tepenia is their country first, and robots holding genuine, unforced appreciation for the humans who chose to give up Upper Earth status to join them. Overwhelmingly positive on both sides, not adversarial or exploitative. Exception: Kunlun and Dome Fuji forbid human presence entirely — not discrimination, pure safety (extreme altitude+cold would kill a human but not a robot), "the way people keep each other out of a minefield, not the way a closed club keeps people out." Robots in Concordia are bisexual by default, universally, not organized by partner gender; human women share this default, human men are heterosexual/fixed-gender-attraction — stated as descriptive, not a value judgment, tied to robots' different relationship to embodiment/identity.
