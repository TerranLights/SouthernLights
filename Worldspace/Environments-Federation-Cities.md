# The Federation's Cities — Environments, Daily Life, and Culture for "Southern Lights"

**Purpose of this document:** reference material for show-planning — what to physically build/scout, what to show characters doing, and what cultural texture to draw on — city by city, across five of Tepenia's six Arcanet subnets (Concordia, the sixth, has its own document: [`Environments-Concordia-Districts.md`](Environments-Concordia-Districts.md)). Compiled from the InnerTepeniaGDD's City Megasheets and subnet Ultra-Megasheets, via three parallel research passes, 2026-07-11.

**Dating tags used throughout:** **[SIP]** = falls within 2564–2812 (the Second Interwar Period), the show's own usable window. **[POST-WAR/GAME-PRESENT]** = specifically tied to the Long Night War (2812) or its aftermath. **[UNDATED]** = era unspecified, often dual-purpose (true both before and after 2812).

See also [`Locations.md`](Locations.md) for each city's founding story, 2812 fate, and subnet grouping at a glance — this document goes deeper on physical environment, daily activity, and culture per city.

# PART ONE: PALMER, BYRD & AMUNDSEN STATION SUBNETS

## Palmer Subnet, Byrd, and Amundsen Station — Environments, Daily Life, and Show-Planning Notes

Source: Inner Tepenia GDD, `Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Outside-World/Tepenian-Federation/Locations/Cities/City_Megasheets/` — Palmer_Subnet (8 cities), Byrd_Subnet (1 city), Amundsen_Station. Compiled for *Southern Lights* (SIP: June 21, 2564 Falkland Treaty → 2812 Long Night War, refugees flee to Concordia).

**Dating tags used throughout:**
- **[SIP]** — usable directly for the show; describes the city/place as living and functioning during the 248-year Second Interwar Period.
- **[POST-WAR/GAME-PRESENT]** — specific to after 2812 (ruin state, refugee diaspora, game's "present day" ~2822–2827). Not show-usable as a present-tense setting, but useful for knowing what NOT to depict as pre-destruction, or for a flash-forward/epilogue.
- **[UNDATED]** — GDD doesn't pin this to a specific point in the 248-year window; treat as usable anywhere in SIP unless noted otherwise.

**Framing note on the whole Palmer subnet:** every one of the eight cities except Signy is either destroyed or damaged by the Long Night War (2812), which is the *terminus* of the show's timeframe, not its setting. That means roughly 248 years of living history is available for the show, and only the final chapter (the war itself, ~2812) touches the destruction. Palmer City and Amundsen Station in particular are **confirmed destroyed** — everything below describing them as functioning places is [SIP] material the show can use freely; only their ruin-state is [POST-WAR].

## 1. Palmer Subnet — Overview

**Real-world basis:** the Antarctic Peninsula and its surrounding island chains — closest part of Antarctica to South America, historically the most contested/densely populated region of the continent (real-world overlapping Argentine/Chilean/British claims).

**The eight member cities:** Palmer City, Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Marambio, Port Lockroy, Rothera, Sejong, Signy. Founded starting the literal day of the Falkland Treaty (June 21, 2564) with Palmer City — the first Tepenian settlement anywhere.

**Geography for blocking/production design:** island-heavy and archipelago-scattered. Five of eight cities (Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Marambio, Sejong, Signy) sit on islands with **no highway access at all** — reachable only by sea or air. This is a maritime world at its edges, a highway-and-industrial world at its center (Rothera, Marambio). A single continental highway, "Hwy 1," threads Esperanza (northern terminus) → Marambio → a ramp to Port Lockroy/Palmer City → a ramp to Rothera → onward out of subnet to Byrd.

**Organizing principle — reachability, not nationality:** Palmer subnet's founding logic, city after city, traces back to being *reachable*: by the first wave of exiles who didn't wait (Palmer City), by South American shipping and Upper Earth tourism (Palmer City again), by bureaucratic necessity (Juan Carlos), as a trans-shipment corridor (Esperanza, Marambio). This is the one subnet where population is not organized by founding nationality — USA ends up demographically Primary or co-Primary almost everywhere by the "Census II" era regardless of who actually founded a city, and several cities (Palmer City, Esperanza) were founded on explicitly non-national compacts. **For the show:** Palmer subnet reads as Tepenia's cosmopolitan gateway — the most demographically mixed, multi-accented, multi-cuisine region, in contrast to more culturally homogeneous interior cities elsewhere in Tepenia.

**Wartime fate (2812), for reference only:** four of eight cities destroyed outright (Palmer City, Esperanza, Juan Carlos, Sejong), one further crippled by dual-asset loss (Marambio, also destroyed), two damaged-but-partially-operational (Port Lockroy, Rothera), and only Signy survived intact — through sheer isolation, which post-war becomes its own vulnerability (a robot-nutrition/siligel supply crisis). **This means Palmer subnet is the best single-subnet illustration, anywhere in Tepenia, of "everything that was lost" — useful as visual and emotional shorthand for what the Long Night War actually took**, if the show wants a late-season gut-punch: showing the audience, across earlier episodes, what these places were like when they were full of people.

## 2. Palmer City — "The one city built purely for joy"

**Status:** [SIP: 2564 founding through 2812] — thriving; **[POST-WAR]** destroyed in the Long Night War, flooded/decaying ruins by game-present. Given the timeline, Palmer City is almost certainly the show's best candidate for its **opening setting / pilot location** — it's literally where Tepenia begins, on Day One of the exile.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Founded on Anvers Island (Palmer Station, USA), reached historically by boat crossing (not on the mainline highway network — its own island geography). Weather is the single most distinctive physical fact about Palmer City in all of Tepenia: **the only Tepenian city where above-freezing temperatures and actual rain are normal, everyday weather.** Streets are "lush with rain, sometimes delicate, sometimes pounding, with sun breaking through unpredictably." A web of interconnecting streets, cultural buildings, dance halls, casinos, concert venues. Proposed named streets/landmarks worth using as location names: **Rue de la Réception** (the ceremonial boulevard for VIP/tourist arrivals), **100 Miles Davis Boulevard** (the historic, lived-in heart, named for the jazz heritage), **The Petrograd Room** (the grandest casino/entertainment hall — imperial, over-the-top, "we willed this into existence and it worked"), the **Little Burgundy Quarter** (the dense live-music neighborhood around the original station buildings, direct homage to Montreal's historic jazz district), and **The Boardwalk** (an elevated walkway built specifically so visitors can enjoy the rare rain/above-freezing weather, doubling as nightlife real estate).

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Economy is roughly: entertainment/hospitality/tourism 45%, commercial/trade 15% (subnet hub), cultural institutions/arts 15%, technical/scientific 10% (inherited Palmer Station research tradition), marine/resource extraction 10%. In practice this means: casino dealers, hostesses, live musicians (jazz, rockabilly, electroswing bands — genuinely a hybrid old/new nightlife scene, not just a jazz museum), hospitality staff, customs/immigration processing for Upper Earth tourists, dockworkers, entertainers. The city runs substantially on **Upper Earth tourism revenue** — Upper Earth officially disdains Tepenia but privately (an open secret on their own side) indulges in visiting for gambling, nightlife, and the freedom to be around openly-personhood-recognized robots without their home country's baggage. Visitors process through **Machu Picchu Base** on King George Island (a separate customs/immigration gateway, distinct from Sejong, the actual Tepenian city on that island) then cross calmer waters to Palmer City itself, understanding that Tepenian law applies to them while present — a quiet, repeated act of sovereignty theater.

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
Jazz is the founding touchstone — literally seeded by phonograph records found in the original station buildings — but the living scene has grown into a broader genre range (rockabilly, electroswing). Two named municipal holidays: **"The Day We Didn't Wait"** (Founding Day, June 21 — commemorating that exiles arrived the same day the treaty was signed, without hesitation) and **"Needle Down"** (a citywide dusk-to-dawn live-music festival celebrating the discovery of the jazz records — explicitly modeled on Montreal's real-world jazz festival, the largest in the world). Founding population arrived as three groups united not by nationality but by *relationship to robots*: robots exiled under treaty, humans in committed relationships with specific robots, and human ideological supporters. This makes Palmer City's civic myth "everyone belongs here, because everyone's people arrived here first" — a generous myth that (per the GDD's own cross-reference work) is more inclusive than the literal founding-wave demographics strictly support, which could be a nice, quiet texture for the show: some residents' families were there day one, some weren't, and the city's civic religion papers over the difference.

### Notable specific locations
- **The Petrograd Room** — grandest casino/venue, site of VIP after-hours gatherings.
- **100 Miles Davis Boulevard** / **Little Burgundy Quarter** — dense live-music streets.
- **The Boardwalk** — elevated walkway, nightlife strip built for the rain.
- **Machu Picchu Base** (King George Island) — the customs/immigration gateway Upper Earth tourists pass through before crossing to Palmer City.

### Strong episode-setting notes
This is the single richest environment in the entire dataset for **the show's inciting historical event**: the Long Night War's spark happened here — an Upper Earth diplomat (proposed name **Aldric Whitmore Sennett**) denying personhood to and assaulting a gynoid entertainer (proposed name **Solenne Vaskarre**), working in a closed VIP setting (plausibly the Petrograd Room), with no witnesses — precisely the kind of private space Palmer City's own hospitality economy constantly creates. This is presented in the GDD as close to *structurally inevitable* given the city's founding logic and economy, not a random tragedy. **This is very plausibly meant to be dramatized as a season-ending or series-ending event given the SIP framing — Palmer City's fall (2812, or its inciting incident sometime within the 248-year span) is the literal reason the show's clock runs out.** Given robot lifespans, Solenne may plausibly *survive*, relocate to Concordia before the destruction, and could appear as a recurring, ageless witness-character across the whole show, carrying the exact weight of "the person who started the war" for the full 248-year span — the GDD flags this explicitly as the single biggest open design fork in all of Palmer City's material, and it maps directly onto the show's own robot-persists/human-generations-cycle structure.

## 3. Esperanza — "The Guarded City" / children, mining, family

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812; [POST-WAR] destroyed (an early casualty, proposed as a deliberate *morale* strike specifically because it was Tepenia's 2nd-largest city and the one most explicitly built around children/hope).

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Hope Bay, Trinity Peninsula — reached via Hwy 1's actual northern terminus. Island/peninsula mining settlement; Tepenia's **second-largest city** (over 1.1 million combined population at Census II). Mines dominate the working landscape; children are audibly and visibly everywhere, "the immediate, unmistakable marker that this isn't an ordinary Tepenian city."

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
This is the strongest "family and childhood" location in the whole dataset. **Fathers work Esperanza's mines on rotational shifts — weeks away, then a week or two home.** Given Tepenia's real demographics (human exile immigration skewed heavily male; children conceived predominantly via normalized artificial-womb technology), the **normal Esperanza household is a father plus a robot caregiver**, not a deficiency or gap. Robots are the primary at-home caregivers during shift absences *and* the primary schoolteachers. Fathers take children fishing at the island's edges when home. Mining supplies raw materials feeding siligel synthesis and construction/fabrication across Tepenia — Esperanza quietly supplies Tepenian expansion in two directions at once: the *people* (a generation explicitly raised to "push further into Antarctica, in the name of robots and humans hand-in-hand") and the *materials*.

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
The founding compact was never a single legal document — it lived as an unwritten but universally understood civic covenant: "any human whose family had made the sacrifice of exile has a claim on what Esperanza exists to protect," open to any nationality. This is echoed by a partially-surviving birth registry (started from a sealed 1978 Argentine military archive found by the earliest settlers) — the diaspora's most treasured surviving artifact fragment. Given robot lifespans and ordinary human ones, "being from Hope" as an identity is carried forward mainly by the **long-lived robot caregivers and teachers**, not human bloodlines — a strong emotional throughline for a multi-generation show, since the same robot character could plausibly have raised multiple successive human generations of the same family.

### Notable specific locations
- The mines (rotational-shift labor, the emotional center of the father/child/robot-caregiver dynamic).
- The fishing edges of the island (father-child bonding time).
- The school (robot-taught).
- The sealed archive room where the original birth registry/founding documents were found.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Excellent for **a slice-of-life, multi-generational family episode**: a robot caregiver raising several successive generations of the same human family across a century-plus, fathers cycling through mining shifts, children growing up not questioning a household structure the audience might initially read as unusual. Also strong for **a frontier/expansion episode** — Esperanza-raised young adults setting off to found or reinforce more remote settlements, explicitly raised for that purpose.

## 4. Juan Carlos — "The Federation's memory," quiet intellectual island

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812; [POST-WAR] destroyed — deliberately targeted specifically *because* of its ongoing archive/intelligence function (a "security strike," no witness required or possible given its isolation).

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Livingston Island, South Shetlands — no highway access, maritime/aviation only, genuinely rugged and glaciated terrain. Sits far enough north to have **neither true polar night nor midnight sun** — a normal-ish light cycle relative to more extreme Tepenian cities, worth noting for cinematography (this isn't one of the "eternal dark" locations).

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Juan Carlos was **Tepenia's first bureaucratic archive** — recording who entered, who left, who applied for Palmer City visas or permanent immigration, and specifically flagging anyone who'd held a position in an Upper Earth home government. This connected directly to the Machu Picchu Border & Customs Authority (whose actual physical processing point is Machu Picchu Airport, near Sejong). Most residents, per the GDD, likely experienced this as ordinary administrative paperwork, without any sense that they were building what would eventually become the seed of the Federation's entire institutional memory (the archive was later relocated deeper into Antarctica and became the basis of Amundsen Station's pre-Split-Brain archive).

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
The defining social institution is the **tertulia** — not an open gathering but a hosted one, with a guest list, an implied topic, and real intellectual expectation: argument pursued as its own worthwhile, joyful civic practice, not something to win or avoid. This is a strong, specific, filmable social ritual — dinner-party-adjacent, verbally combative but affectionate, a recurring venue for exposition/relationship drama. Proposed civic self-image, half-joking: **"the Federation's memory"** — residents proud, in a wry Hay-on-Wye-"Kingdom of Books" register, of a record-keeping identity that outlasted the archive's own literal relocation.

### Notable specific locations
- The archive itself (before its relocation to Amundsen Station) — records room, ledgers, visa stamps, the literal seed of Tepenian bureaucracy.
- Whatever household or hall hosts the tertulia — recurring "salon" location for verbal sparring, argument, gossip, political undercurrents.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Good for **a quiet, dialogue-driven "salon" episode** — the tertulia tradition is a ready-made recurring set piece (like a standing dinner party) where major exposition, political tension, or personal drama could unfold through hosted argument. Also a strong choice for a **bureaucracy/paper-trail mystery** episode — a clerk or archivist character quietly realizing the scale of what they're building, or a visiting Upper Earth-born character being tracked/flagged by the very system Juan Carlos invented.

## 5. Marambio — dual air/sea logistics hub, fossil beds, dockworkers

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812; [POST-WAR] destroyed — the strike eliminated both its airfield and its (later-recognized) shipyard in one blow, given Seymour Island's small, flat, concentrated geography.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Seymour Island, Weddell Sea — flat, exposed, island terrain, dominated visually by the airfield. Reached by Hwy 1 via the **Picnic Passage causeway/bridge chain** (a 0.93km crossing) — a filmable, specific piece of infrastructure. Equally important but less obvious on first glance: shipyards on the island's inner side, facing the mainland.

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Marambio is a **dual-mode logistics powerhouse** — air (one of only eight functional airports in all of Tepenia) and sea (a genuine shipyard) simultaneously. Humans and robots load and unload ships together "in any weather, rain or clear skies or snow, work continuing regardless." During quiet stretches between dockings, humans and robots take **smoke breaks side by side**. Many human dockworkers are in relationships with robot coworkers — an ordinary, lived version of Tepenia's human-robot "Bonded Lattice" theme; some workers are here specifically because robot society treated them better than human society ever did. Marambio handles **bulk, high-volume, less-differentiated cargo**, distinct from Esperanza's smaller, specific/documented shipments (the concrete lore example: a named artisan's whalebone-and-metal pianos shipped through Esperanza specifically, not Marambio).

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
Institutional discipline inherited from the Argentine Air Force's original 1969 base, persisting culturally as an operational rigor even as Argentina's own founding-nation demographic presence eroded to almost nothing by Census II (0.74%, the deepest founder-dilution documented anywhere in the subnet) — an "inherited institutional culture that outlived its founders," worth a beat if the show wants to show a workplace whose formal, disciplined culture feels oddly out of step with who's actually working there generations later.

### Notable specific locations
- **Seymour Island's exposed sedimentary cliffs/beds** — 34-56 million-year-old Eocene fossils, proof of a warmer, forested pre-ice Antarctica, sitting right alongside the working airfield and docks. A striking visual juxtaposition: heavy industrial infrastructure built directly on top of/next to deep-time geological evidence. Good location for a paleontologist or scientifically-curious character subplot.
- **Picnic Passage** causeway/bridge chain.
- The shipyard's smoke-break spot — informal, human-scale gathering point amid industrial scale.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Marambio is the best pure "working port/blue-collar industrial daily life" location in the subnet — ideal for episodes centered on dockworkers, cross-species workplace romance, and the physical rhythm of loading/unloading regardless of weather. Also good for a quieter subplot about the fossil beds (a scientist character, or simply a visual motif of "the ground remembers a warmer world" playing against the Antarctic setting).

## 6. Port Lockroy — heritage town, postal relay, "Old Toby"

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812; [POST-WAR] damaged, partially operational (survived via strategic irrelevance/likely misidentification with Palmer City's larger strike zone — the "too insignificant to bother with" survival, the precise inverse of Kyoto's WWII sparing).

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Goudier Island/Wiencke Island. **Unique founding progression** unlike any other Tepenian city: military installation (1944 Operation Tabarin) → heritage museum → living exile city. Visibly **layered architecture** — the original 1944 buildings incorporated directly into later construction rather than demolished. A sheltered harbor. Tepenia's 2nd-smallest city (~96,000 combined population).

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Two intertwined economic roles: harbor/maritime trade (the "full waystation," slower and richer) and a growing **postal/correspondence specialization** — Port Lockroy is the Palmer subnet's word-and-archive counterpart to Rothera's material-freight role ("Rothera moves the materials, Port Lockroy moves the words"). The GDD's single richest character vignette in the entire Palmer subnet dataset is set here: a gruff, grizzled old man in a sailor's coat and hat — proposed name **Tobias "Old Toby" Fenwick** — tending goats, handing a parcel to a robot courier at the main postal office mid-chore, then picking up hammer and chisel to keep working on a stone wall he'll never finish. He smokes Argentine-imported tobacco (Antarctic hydroponics still rudimentary this early in the SIP). He muses the city might someday become a postal hub — doubts he'll live to see it, and is right, but not wrong about where it's headed (the postal role does mature into "real and fully functioning" by roughly 2650s-2750s).

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
The **communal stone wall** — a multi-generational artifact, other residents adding their own carved panels over decades/centuries, until it physically embodies the city's whole "layered, nothing-demolished" identity. Two proposed holidays: **Founders' Watch** (a literal overnight vigil re-enacting the original 1944 wartime watch for German naval activity) and **The Long Memory** (the wall-honoring holiday, whose central ritual act is literally adding a new panel to the wall). Founding tension here isn't national, it's *temporal*: is this city's history something lived inside, or something performed for heritage-museum visitors?

### Notable specific locations
- **The communal stone wall** — an ideal recurring visual/location motif across the whole 248-year span: different actors/generations of the same family could plausibly be shown adding panels to the same wall in different episodes/timeframes, visually compressing centuries.
- **The main postal office** — where Old Toby exchanges parcels with robot couriers.
- The goat pen.
- The original 1944 Operation Tabarin structures, incorporated into later buildings.

### Strong episode-setting notes
The stone wall device is genuinely one of the strongest visual through-lines available anywhere in this research for a 248-year ensemble show — a single physical object that literally accumulates the marks of different generations, could open or close multiple episodes across different eras. Old Toby himself is a strong "found family / small-town elder" character type — mundane daily labor (goats, mail, masonry) juxtaposed with a quietly correct prophecy about his city's future that he won't live to see fulfilled. Good contained, slow, character-driven episode setting (contrast to Palmer City's spectacle or Marambio's industrial scale).

## 7. Rothera — decentralized industrial "workshop," underground vaults

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812; [POST-WAR] damaged, partially operational — survived specifically because its factories/infrastructure were never concentrated in one place (deliberate decentralization + genuine underground vault shelters), a rare case of a city proud specifically of *why* it survived.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Adelaide Island, Marguerite Bay — approached via the dramatic **Lemaire Channel**, "one of the most dramatic maritime passages in Antarctica," explicitly proposed as Rothera's cinematic establishing shot/entry sequence. Not one dense urban core but a **decentralized industrial landscape spread across a much larger physical scale than any other Peninsula city** — stretches of fabrication yards, residential areas interspersed, advanced forklifts hauling materials constantly. Beneath the surface: **large-scale excavated underground vault sections**, bigger than typical small post-apocalyptic-style shelters, made possible by Adelaide Island's abundant size, built up gradually over generations and doubling as genuine wartime population shelters.

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Rothera is the Palmer subnet's **industrial spine** — moving materials (vehicle and transit-infrastructure components, general construction goods) via Hwy 1 to every other Palmer subnet city, mirroring the same role Mirny plays for its own subnet. Humans run 8–10 hour shifts, robots 10–12 hour shifts. **After-shift culture:** everyone stops at one of a variety of local bars/pubs — humans drink alcohol, robots drink "glitch-coolant" — described as a working-class, limited-selection-but-stronger-effect bar scene (shared with Byrd), matching the city's function-over-presentation civic identity. A rare functional runway (one of only eight in Tepenia) serves Peninsula-internal flights, secondary to Marambio's longer-range aviation hub role.

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
Two proposed holidays: **Founders' Airstrip** (maintenance-crew open house, civic ceremony built around the inherited runway/institutional competence) and **The Standing Works** (marking the city's continued post-war industrial function — though notably this reads as *quiet, earned pride*, not mourning, even on the anniversary of the war's strikes — worth remembering if the show ever needs a post-timeframe epilogue beat).

### Notable specific locations
- **The Lemaire Channel** — sea approach, dramatic establishing shot.
- The decentralized fabrication yards spread across the island.
- The underground vault network (multi-generational excavation project, doubling as shelter).
- The after-shift bar scene — informal, working-class, cross-species social hub.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Best "blue-collar industrial city" location alongside Marambio, but with a more sprawling, dispersed physical geography (good for chase/hide sequences across a large working landscape) and the underground vaults as a distinct, separate location register (claustrophobic, generational, protective). The after-work bar culture (human alcohol / robot glitch-coolant, side by side) is a strong recurring social setting for ensemble scenes.

## 8. Sejong — dense vertical multinational mini-city ("mini-Taipei")

**Status:** [SIP] thriving through 2812, an ongoing and never-resolved "coexistence experiment"; [POST-WAR] destroyed — proposed as a fourth, distinct strike doctrine: an "ideological erasure strike," destroying Sejong specifically *before* its multinational cooperation experiment could be judged a success, since a celebrated example of exactly that kind of cooperation would embarrass Upper Earth's own narrative that Tepenia's exiles couldn't build anything real.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
King George Island, South Shetlands — no highway (too far from the mainland for any bridge), maritime/aviation-access only. Explicitly described as "something like **Taipei at a much smaller scale**" — dense and vertical rather than sprawling, since the island's land constraints forced building up, not out. Infrastructure anchored directly into solid bedrock (a structural contrast with ice-shelf cities that must design around constantly-moving ground). High-rise buildings cluster together for practical wind protection. A proposed defining landmark: **the Lighthouse Spire** — a combined lighthouse-and-signal tower, the tallest structure on the island, visible from every national community's own footholds, functioning as a literal shared civic landmark none of the dozen resident communities can claim exclusively. City geometry is proposed as wheel-like: the neutral Spire at the hub, each national community's territory a spoke radiating outward, with architecturally-transitional "boundary zones" as the seams between adjacent spokes.

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Fishing is a real economic pillar; much of the cuisine is seafood-based. The city's actual civic art form is **constant, informal, many-sided diplomatic coordination** — a skill built from sheer necessity, continuously renegotiating the boundary with whichever neighbor happens to be adjacent. This is the most demographically crowded single-island setting in all of Palmer subnet: nearly a dozen distinct national communities (Korean-founding plus Argentine, Brazilian, Chinese, Polish, Russian, Czech, Uruguayan, and more) sharing one small island in permanent, negotiated proximity, absorbed into Sejong's own civic structure as distinct neighborhoods rather than separate municipalities.

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP]
Two proposed holidays: **Hangul Day** (commemorating King Sejong the Great's historical creation of the Korean alphabet — the uncontested, shared civic celebration, since it honors the city's own namesake rather than any one resident community's heritage) and **The Neighbors' Accord** (marking the anniversary of the original land-use/resource-sharing agreement that made coexistence physically workable — explicitly a celebration of *practical* success, deliberately separate from any claim the underlying social competitive tension was ever fully resolved). The coexistence experiment succeeds functionally (shared infrastructure, daily logistics) while never fully resolving emotionally/socially — "the mild climate... paradoxically made it more competitive: multiple communities vying for the same favorable ground." This unresolved tension is real, ongoing dramatic material, not backstory.

### Notable specific locations
- **The Lighthouse Spire** — shared civic landmark, meeting point, possible site for cross-national diplomacy/romance/conflict scenes.
- The boundary zones between national quarters — transitional, negotiated space, good for scenes about identity and belonging.
- Fishing docks/seafood markets.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Sejong is the strongest location in the dataset for **a multi-ethnic, close-quarters ensemble episode about diplomacy, rivalry, and coexistence under real physical density** — nearly a dozen national identities sharing one small island, constant informal boundary renegotiation, and a genuine open question (never resolved before the war) about whether such a place can actually work long-term. Excellent contrast piece against Juan Carlos (same starting circumstances, opposite outcome — comparative isolation vs. density) if the show wants a matched-pair episode structure.

## 9. Signy — remote, wildlife-paced, two islands and one bridge

**Status:** [SIP] thriving throughout, genuinely the **only Palmer subnet city that survives the Long Night War fully intact** (with a real post-war asterisk: a robot-nutrition/siligel supply crisis from being cut off, [POST-WAR] only). Because Signy survives, its "present tense, ongoing" identity is not confined to a specific pre-2812 window — the show could plausibly show Signy at any point across the SIP and, unusually among these cities, even briefly gesture at its state immediately after 2812 without it reading as destruction footage.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
South Orkney Islands, Scotia Sea — Tepenia's most isolated city, no highway, maritime-access only, the weakest Arcanet link in the Federation (relies on old-fashioned **radio**, not digital networking, because modern networking physically can't reach this far). Genuine two-island structure: **Signy Island** (smaller, hosts the one shipping dock on its east side and a substantial radio comms setup) and **Coronation Island** (much larger, most residential living and leisure infrastructure), connected by **a single high bridge** — proposed name **the Endurance Span**, honoring Shackleton/St. Ernest, whose historic 1916 open-boat journey passed within ~300km of these islands.

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
The defining daily rhythm: humans and robots wake (or power on) and travel south across the bridge to the docking island to fish, returning in the afternoon to Coronation Island's downtime — a literal daily echo of the Endurance crew's own crossing-for-survival, just safe and domesticated. **Floating extension-platforms** expand fishing reach out to sea wherever geography allows. "Time moves slowly here — nobody's in a real rush, a civic rhythm paced by wildlife cycles rather than urgency." Marine resources are abundant enough to sustain the human population on a purely marine diet indefinitely (a genuine post-war asset, contrasted with the robot siligel shortage that has no local substitute).

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP/some UNDATED]
The strongest **St. Ernest (Shackleton) veneration claim anywhere in Tepenia** — the historic Endurance route runs right past these islands. Two proposed holidays: **The Long Watch** (built around patient, unhurried endurance — plausibly marking the arrival of the season's first supply ship, historically a day the community waited for) and **St. Ernest's Passage** (directly commemorating the Endurance open-boat journey, observed at the bridge itself). A unique South African founding-wave demographic presence (via the Cape Town–South Orkneys route) — the only Palmer subnet city with this particular arrival thread, worth noting for casting/accent variety.

### Notable specific locations
- **The Endurance Span** — the single bridge connecting the two islands; a strong recurring visual and ritual site (daily commute, annual observance).
- The docking/fishing island (Signy Island) — labor side.
- Coronation Island's residential/leisure side — downtime, home life.
- Floating fishing platforms at sea.

### Strong episode-setting notes
The best "quiet, slow, unhurried" location in the whole dataset — genuinely wildlife-paced daily rhythm, ideal for a bottle episode or a season's emotional palate-cleanser, in contrast to Palmer City's spectacle or Sejong's density. Could also work as an "outsider visits the one place the war never touched" episode if the show ever wants to show a character briefly escaping the war's shadow elsewhere in the subnet. Since Signy is the one Palmer-subnet city that *isn't* destroyed, it's also a plausible place to root a recurring or long-running character/family thread that persists visually consistent across the whole 248-year span without requiring "ruins" imagery.

## 10. Byrd (Byrd Subnet) — underground mega-city, "built from below, then left alone"

**Status:** [SIP + POST-WAR both viable] — Byrd **survives the Long Night War intact** and remains one of only three Tepenian cities still functioning at meaningful scale afterward (alongside Concordia and Janbogo). Unlike the Palmer subnet cities, Byrd's story is not bookended by destruction — it's a continuously viable setting across the entire show timeframe and beyond. Sole city in its entire subnet (the Byrd/"Pacific" subnet) — carries all the narrative complexity other subnets spread across 4-8 cities.

### Physical environment & architecture [UNDATED, spans SIP]
Marie Byrd Land, West Antarctica — founded by an all-robot expedition crew (the risk was judged too great for humans) driving Tepenia's first native tracked overland vehicles, the **Arrastradoras** (worn down over time to "Rastra") — a genuine "founded by robots" origin story distinct from every other Tepenian city. The expedition found not a surface station but **a field of compacted snow over buried, already-abandoned infrastructure** — no surface structure to discover, only old maps and manifests. The community built downward before it ever built up: **surface reads as vast icefields with huge warehouses and a massive trucking depot as the only visible landscape features, giant elevators leading down** to where the actual city lives. The underground scale is "staggering on first encounter for what looks, from above, like a comparatively small settlement." Deepest, earliest-buried layers plausibly predate the Tepenian exile community's own arrival entirely — genuine pre-exile, Upper Earth-era material sitting under centuries of subsequent Tepenian construction (a literal physical connection to Upper Earth's own historical presence in Antarctica, unknown to most current residents).

### Daily activities & occupations [UNDATED, spans SIP]
Byrd is one of only **two active "Cradle" sites in all of Tepenia** — i.e., one of only two places that can still manufacture the fabrication-synthesis chambers that physically create robots, a genuinely singular occupation/industry unique to this city (and Soyuz, in a different subnet). Below the icefield: huge mechanized fabrication plants, chamber-works, import/export dispatch offices. Byrd's own founding-story vehicle lineage (Rastra-descended heavy haulers) plausibly is manufactured here today, carrying everything Byrd exports out along its two remaining routes: **Hwy 1** north to the Peninsula/Palmer subnet and onward toward South America, and **Hwy 22** east across the continent through the (later-destroyed) Amundsen Station site to the Mirny subnet. Byrd's own aviation fleet broke down and was never repaired (proposed cause: a specific material/component/fuel-additive Byrd can't produce locally, tied to a now-severed supply corridor — not a skills failure, since Byrd's whole identity is fabrication competence) — this sealed off Byrd's own refugee population in Concordia as a permanently closed community, no new arrivals or departures, a strong, specific piece of diaspora texture if the show ever cuts to Concordia. Proposed five internal "guild" factions, useful as recurring institutional texture/set-pieces: **The Fabricators' Guild** (chamber-works, the city's identity core), **The Dispatch Office** (controls the trucking depot, everything entering/leaving via the two highways), **The Foundation Keepers** (snow/structural management, controls access to the buried sections), **The Prospectors' Circle** (resource extraction/geology, watches West Antarctic ice-sheet instability), **The Grounded Wings** (salvage/aviation maintenance — tending a fleet that can't fly, holding institutional memory of why).

### Cultural practices & rituals [UNDATED]
After-shift bar culture shared structurally with Rothera (working-class, limited-selection-but-stronger-effect bars, humans on alcohol/robots on glitch-coolant). Proposed **St. Richard's** civic veneration site at the original surface encampment location — where the first arrivals stood before they knew what lay beneath them, honoring the moment of uncertainty just before the discovery that defined the city (a marker/memorial rather than a preserved structure). Demographically, Byrd inherited an enormous, ~34-nation "Notable tier" diversity one step removed from Palmer City's own population overflow (via the Framheim/Little America redistribution) — meaning a tight, expedition-minded, self-sufficient founding culture now houses nearly as much demographic breadth as Tepenia's single most diverse city (Palmer City itself), a real tension worth playing if the show ever sets scenes here.

### Notable specific locations
- **The elevators** down from the icefield surface warehouses/depot into the buried city — the core visual "reveal" of Byrd (surface looks small and sparse; underground is a staggering mega-city).
- **The Chamber Works** — one of only two places on the planet that can build a robot from nothing; an obviously powerful setting for any robot-origin storyline given the show's ageless robot characters.
- The original surface encampment (St. Richard's site).
- The deepest, oldest buried layers — pre-exile, Upper Earth-era material, essentially archaeological within the show's own setting.
- The trucking depot / two-directional highway network.

### Strong episode-setting notes
Byrd is the standout candidate for a **robot-origin-story episode** — since it's one of only two places in the setting where new robot bodies/chambers are actually made, and since the show is anchored by ageless robot characters, a flashback or present-day episode set in Byrd's Chamber Works could meaningfully explain where one of the show's robot leads actually "came from." The underground-city reveal (sparse icefield surface → staggering buried mega-city via elevator) is a strong visual set-piece for an entrance/establishing sequence. The five-guild internal structure gives a workplace/political ensemble a home without needing multiple cities. Because Byrd is one of the few settings that survives the entire 248-year span intact, it's also a strong candidate for a location that literally doesn't change much in appearance across widely-separated episodes/timeframes — useful if the show wants a stable "home base" location contrasted against a Palmer subnet that keeps changing/getting destroyed around it.

## 11. Amundsen Station — the neutral relay, "not a city"

**Status:** [SIP through 2812, destroyed 2812] — explicitly **not a city** in the GDD's own framing: a strategic infrastructure installation (the neutral inter-subnet Arcanet relay and the base of the "Amundsen Tower," a space-elevator-like structure) staffed by **rotating multi-subnet technical crews** who served a tour and went home, not a permanent settled population. No founding-population-vs-majority dynamic, no generational culture. **Destroyed in the Long Night War** — its ruins are the single most significant ruin site in all of Tepenia, and its loss ("the Planetary Split Brain") permanently severed inter-subnet Arcanet communication, i.e., it's the literal moment Tepenia's six subnets lost the ability to talk to each other.

### Physical environment & architecture [SIP]
Geographic South Pole, ~2,835m elevation — real-world basis is Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station. Population was ~84% robot (the highest robot proportion of any Tepenian population center) — only ~1,100 humans against ~5,700 robots pre-war. Because of this, the built environment was never designed as a human settlement with equipment added — it was fundamentally **a machine facility that happened to accommodate a small human presence**: human quarters "sized for a rotating technical crew... and it likely showed," cuisine almost entirely imported and rationed. Physically: genuinely cramped, every surface covered in cabling, racks, dense equipment (real-world touchstones cited: the ISS interior, Antarctic ice-core processing labs, NASA Mission Control's wall-of-consoles aesthetic, nuclear power plant control rooms, nuclear submarine interiors — all "extreme technical density in confined space"). **The Amundsen Tower's actual primary functions were energy regulation (a flywheel mechanism) and bulk material export, with passenger transport secondary** — i.e., visually this is a piece of heavy infrastructure, not primarily a transit terminal.

### Daily activities & occupations [SIP]
Life here was the specific, intense, temporary camaraderie of a rotating technical crew sharing extreme isolation — not a deep civic culture built across generations. Its single defining civic trait: **structural political neutrality** — the one place belonging to all six subnets and therefore to none, where inter-subnet disputes too difficult to resolve internally were sometimes brought for impartial mediation. Effectively a company-town/military-outpost register rather than a hometown register: staff came from all six subnets on rotation, formed intense short-term bonds, then rotated home carrying the experience with them for the rest of (a very long, if robot) their lives.

### Cultural practices & rituals [SIP, mostly UNDATED — thin by design]
No true settled civic culture (deliberately, per GDD design intent) — but a meaningful implication: given robots make up ~84% of the population and live for centuries, **individual robot characters who once rotated through Amundsen Station on a tour could plausibly still be alive anywhere across all six subnets**, carrying firsthand memory of the pre-destruction relay station and the world before the Planetary Split Brain. This is an excellent hook for the show's ageless-robot ensemble structure: any long-lived robot character could plausibly have a "I served a tour at the Pole once, before it fell" beat, without needing to be a main character *at* Amundsen Station.

### Notable specific locations
- The Arcanet relay control room (NASA-Mission-Control-style wall of consoles) — the literal switching hardware for Tepenia's continent-wide communications.
- The Amundsen Tower's base — flywheel/energy-regulation and bulk-cargo machinery.
- The Geographic South Pole ceremonial marker (needed periodic repositioning even before the war, due to ice drift).
- Dense equipment corridors/maintenance access spaces (claustrophobic, submarine-like).

### Strong episode-setting notes
Amundsen Station is less a "daily life" location and more a **pressure-cooker, limited-cast, high-stakes setting** — a small rotating crew, extreme isolation, dense machine environment, structurally neutral ground where disputes get mediated. Strong candidate for a **bottle episode about inter-subnet politics/diplomacy** (representatives from multiple subnets meeting on genuinely neutral ground) or for the show's version of a "control room during a crisis" episode. Given the destruction here is the literal event that permanently severs Tepenia's communications network, **this is probably the show's best candidate for depicting a specific, dated catastrophic event within the 2564-2812 window** (assuming the show wants to dramatize the Long Night War's actual military mechanics anywhere) — the collapse of the Tower is described as inseparably taking down both the physical cable/space-elevator infrastructure and the relay hardware at once, a single catastrophic structural failure rather than a slow siege.

## 12. Cross-City / Cross-Subnet Relationships

**Trade and logistics — a real internal division of labor, useful for showing characters/goods moving between locations:**
- **Rothera moves materials** — vehicle/transit-infrastructure components and construction goods, distributed via Hwy 1 to every other Palmer subnet city.
- **Port Lockroy moves words** — correspondence and archival material, the postal/relay counterpart to Rothera's freight.
- **Marambio moves bulk volume** — high-volume, less-differentiated cargo via its dual air-and-sea hub.
- **Esperanza moves documented, specific shipments** — smaller-scale, higher-value, individually tracked cargo (the concrete example: a named artisan's whalebone-and-metal pianos, shipped through Esperanza's harbor specifically).
Together these four cities form a complete, non-redundant internal logistics network — goods, words, bulk, and specificity — useful if the show wants to track a specific object or letter moving across several episodes/locations.

**Palmer City ↔ Byrd:** Palmer City (with Rothera) contributed historical records/maps that helped the all-robot Belgrano-led expedition locate Byrd Station in the first place; Rothera's own decentralized industrial model plausibly shaped Byrd's early industrial planning. Because Palmer City is destroyed and Byrd is not, **Byrd's own archives may hold the only surviving primary-source material about pre-war Palmer City anywhere in Tepenia** — a strong "the past survives somewhere unexpected" plot device if the show wants to recover lost Palmer City history via a Byrd-set scene.

**Sejong ↔ Juan Carlos:** a clean "matched pair" — both began as exile settlements on isolated South Shetland research stations under near-identical founding circumstances; the one variable that differed was neighbor density (Juan Carlos comparatively alone; Sejong crowded with nearly a dozen national communities). This single variable produced Juan Carlos's settled archival institution versus Sejong's constant informal multilateral diplomacy — a strong structural contrast for a two-hander or "same starting point, different outcome" episode pairing.

**Signy's isolation, a double edge:** the same remoteness that spared Signy from Long Night War targeting is also what leaves it hardest to reach for help afterward (weak Arcanet/radio-only link) when its post-war robot-nutrition crisis hits — worth remembering as dramatic irony if the show ever depicts Signy's post-2812 state.

**Byrd's long lifeline east:** Byrd's eastern highway (Hwy 22) runs directly through the Amundsen Station site en route to the rest of the continent — meaning Byrd's entire eastward trade relies on some passage through or around the ruins of the very installation whose destruction cut Tepenia's communications in half. A literal physical route through the show's most significant ruin site, useful for any road-trip / courier-journey episode connecting the two locations.

**Byrd's Concordia diaspora — closed and unreachable:** because Byrd's aviation route died and was never fixed, Byrd's refugee population in Concordia (a real, if underdeveloped, concentration in at least three Concordia districts per the GDD) is a **permanently closed community — no new arrivals, no departures, ever since**. A strong, specific piece of found-family/separated-family material if the show ever wants a Concordia-set character with an unreachable hometown.

**Shared civic mythology across Palmer subnet:** several cities independently show the identical underlying pattern — a founding institution or population *outliving* the demographic group that founded it (Marambio's Argentine Air Force discipline surviving Argentina's own near-total demographic dilution; the general "robots persist where human institutions/memory/bloodlines cannot" pattern recurring independently in Esperanza's caregivers, Marambio's dockworker relationships, and Palmer City's Solenne Vaskarre). **This robots-as-continuity-mechanism pattern is arguably the single most useful cross-cutting theme for the show's own structure** (ageless robots anchoring the story while human generations cycle through) — it is independently, repeatedly re-derived by the GDD itself across unrelated cities, which suggests it's a robust, intentional throughline of the wider setting rather than a coincidence.

Everything above comes from "Ultra-Megasheet" worldbuilding synthesis documents that explicitly label themselves **proposals for review, not locked canon** — particularly the "Full Extrapolation" files, which fill open questions with the compilers' own best-guess answers (flagged accordingly throughout, e.g., proposed names, proposed holiday meanings, proposed destruction mechanisms). ## HALLEY SUBNET

Real-world basis: Queen Maud Land, East Antarctica — coastal ice shelves, inland nunataks (isolated rock outcrops poking through ice), and mountain ranges. Historically hosted British, German, Norwegian, Belgian, Argentine, South African, Russian, Finnish, and Swedish research stations within one geographically compact area — the most nation-diverse founding pattern of any Tepenian subnet. Population at Census II (orbital era, i.e. later in the SIP): ~6.88 million combined (humans + robots) across the 8 cities.

**Subnet-wide texture worth noting for scene-setting [SIP]:** Halley subnet is defined by unstable ground and radically different civic answers to it — Halley keeps moving, Neumayer measures and engineers around instability, Abowasa simply has solid bedrock and doesn't need a trick. The subnet's economic backbone is a literal supply chain: freight lands at Sanay's port, splits toward Troll's airfield or overland along "Hwy 7," and reaches the interior cities from there. This is a physically legible logistics geography a show could track a shipment (or a person) through, city to city.

---

### HALLEY (the city)

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** A city that continuously *moves*. Built on the Brunt Ice Shelf, which drifts ~400–700 meters a year toward the sea; Halley's architecture is modular, with each module mounted on skis fitted with tracks that actively dig into the ice and propel the structure toward the mainland, permanently staying ahead of the shelf's own seaward creep — not a crisis evacuation, a continuous, deliberate crawl. No fixed docks, no airstrip, by deliberate civic choice (the ground would just carry any fixed infrastructure out to sea eventually). Day-to-day transport is by snowmobile — humans and robots riding together across the municipal grounds. Old abandoned modules and positions are visible out on the ice at varying distances behind the current city, a literal trail of the city's own history strung out across the ice shelf. All resupply is overland trucking via Belgrano or Sanay along Hwy 7.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Snowmobile commuting as ordinary transit. Ongoing maintenance-crew work on the ski/track propulsion systems (a visible, physical, blue-collar engineering job — someone is always out on the ice servicing a module's undercarriage). Civic surveying and position-logging work tied to "Position Day" (below) — literally measuring how far the city has traveled. A recurring civic conversation about *when and where to relocate next* — town-hall-style deliberation is baked into daily/seasonal life, not an emergency measure.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **The Crossing** — a holiday commemorating the founding generation's arrival onto the ice shelf itself, before there was a city to move yet.
- **Position Day** — an annual ritual that is simultaneously a practical land-survey exercise and a civic ceremony: recording exactly how far the city has traveled from its original founding site. Because Halley's old sites are eventually carried out to sea and lost (unlike other Tepenian cities' visitable ruins), the Position Day record may be the *only* surviving trace of the city's full geographic history — a strong "the archive is everything, there is no ruin to visit" story hook.
- Civic character: consensus-based, confident, "trades concrete control for something less tangible" (Halley literally built the Sanay subnet's technical nerve-center by hand and let Sanay/the subnet keep the naming credit) — a place whose self-image runs on collective confidence, with (per Full Extrapolation) *no formal contingency plan* for what happens if that confidence ever fails. That absence is flagged in-world as the real vulnerability.

**Notable specific locations:** the trailing line of abandoned modules on the ice (visitable, decaying set-piece); the connector road to Hwy 7; Hwy 59 (also a former Arcanet cable corridor, now purely physical, its southern terminus dead since Amundsen Station's destruction).

**Strong episode settings:** A "moving day" episode — the town-hall deliberation over relocation, intercut with physical work of detaching/repositioning a module. A generational episode returning to an abandoned module from decades ago, now near the calving edge, about to be lost forever — last chance to salvage anything. A Position Day ceremony as a framing device for flashback/memory (surveying = remembering).

**[POST-WAR]:** Newer/more recently relocated modules remain mobile; older modules (less recently engineered) are stuck in place and damaged, sitting closer to the calving edge — the inverse of most Tepenian "old core survives" damage patterns, because Halley's resilience mechanism is active (rewards recency) not passive (rewards age). Total dependence on Belgrano/Sanay overland resupply means Halley's day-to-day survival becomes a hostage to the Troll Airfield conflict (see Cross-Subnet section) — a visible, physical barometer (do the supply trucks arrive this week or not) for a conflict happening in a different city entirely.

### NEUMAYER

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** Built directly on the floating Ekström Ice Shelf. Hydraulic-leg architecture — legs that can be raised as snow accumulates — extended from the original real-world research station's design into the whole city's built character. Visually: a city standing on stilts/legs over ice, adjusting its own height. Engineering labs and outdoor field-testing sites run around the clock on deliberately staggered, overlapping shifts (built specifically so nobody burns out) — a workplace that never fully goes dark.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** R&D and field-testing work — this is a functioning engineering testbed city, not a museum-quiet observatory. Precision instrumentation and measurement are treated as near-sacred civic virtue ("Measured, Not Debated"): survey crews, atmospheric/climate monitoring (a centuries-long unbroken data record, backed up redundantly off-site as routine practice, long before any war). After hours: a real bar/music-venue scene — Electronic, Metal, and Digital-Industrial genres, machinery-adjacent and precision-coded, a good source of source music/soundscape and a hangout location distinct from a "cute Antarctic town" cliché.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **Founders' Instrument** — commemorating the founding generation's decision to treat inherited scientific/engineering equipment as an ongoing mission.
- **The Schematic** — a bittersweet holiday marking the day the Amundsen Tower's design work was formally handed off to its actual construction site (the South Pole) — quietly bittersweet because almost nobody outside Neumayer remembers what the holiday actually commemorates.
- Civic character: meticulous private record-keeping over public credit-seeking. Neumayer is, across three separate eras of its own history, the uncredited engineering author behind (1) the "Rastra" — the vehicle every Tepenian drives, originally a crude Belgrano hauler that Neumayer's founding-era engineers reverse-engineered and redesigned; (2) the Amundsen Tower's structural schematics (the nation's greatest engineering achievement); (3) the current-generation fabrication-synthesis chamber design used at the Cradle's robot-creation manufacturing sites. None of the three carries Neumayer's name. This is played entirely straight in-world — pride and quiet frustration processed through *keeping an exact private archive*, not campaigning for credit.
- A robot drinking/coolant culture defined by precisely calibrated, reproducible effects rather than variety — "Measured, Not Debated" applied to nightlife.

**Notable specific locations:** the engineering labs/field-test sites (visually distinct — instruments out on the ice, staggered-shift lighting at all hours); the bar/music-venue district; Neumayer's internal archive vault (unofficial, but the emotional heart of "nobody knows we built that").

**Strong episode settings:** A robot character quietly revealed to have redesigned something world-changing (the Tower, the vehicle, the chamber) that everyone else takes for granted — an ideal vehicle for an ageless robot character's perspective across generations, since only Neumayer's own internal records would know. A night-shift-to-morning-shift handoff at a lab as a structural device for a two-hander scene. A music-venue episode using the Electronic/Metal/Digital-Industrial scene as texture for a generational-gap or found-family plot.

**[POST-WAR]:** Damaged, partially operational; ice shelf movement continues, monitored precisely, no confirmed loss of any section to calving as of the record.

### TROLL

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP → POST-WAR]:** Centered entirely on Troll Airfield, a 3,000-meter intercontinental-capable runway — one of only eight functioning airports in all of Tepenia. This is a genuine intermodal freight hub: goods land at Sanay or Belgrano, truck overland via Hwy 7, and fly out from Troll to the rest of the country. A major working-class city with above-average spending power thanks to the freight economy; its glitch-coolant culture (working-class, strong-effect, limited-selection) is notably higher-quality than the national standard — modest but well-made, not showy.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Cargo handling, trucking dispatch, runway/tower operations, fuel depot work — an entire city organized around the physical mechanics of freight moving through it. This is a great setting for "blue-collar logistics" characters: dispatchers, ground crew, mechanics.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **St. Roald's Landing** — an older, quieter holiday honoring Amundsen (Norwegian founding heritage, the 1939 Queen Maud Land claim).
- **Runway Day** — a newer, dominant holiday celebrating the airfield itself — the "whoever holds the runway" identity that has increasingly displaced the older Norwegian-heritage observance. The contrast between the two holidays is a clean, visual way to show a city's identity shifting generation to generation — old-timers quietly keeping St. Roald's Landing, the wider city throwing itself into Runway Day.

**Notable specific locations:** the runway and control tower; the fuel depot; the trucking dispatch yard; grounded/partially-maintained cargo aircraft in hangars (a good junk/scavenge location).

**Strong episode settings:** [POST-WAR] Troll Airfield's control is genuinely fractured among multiple factions post-2812 — one holds the runway/tower, another the fuel depot, another the trucking dispatch, no single authority holds it all. This is explicitly the GDD's own flagged "central conflict" location for the game's DLC 5 — for the show, it's a ready-made siege/standoff/heist location: characters from different factions cooperating out of necessity to keep cargo moving even while nominally in conflict. A "last flight out" evacuation episode. A dual-holiday episode contrasting generations' relationship to Norwegian founding identity vs. airfield-defined identity.

### PRINCESS ELISABETH — [DESTROYED in the Long Night War; everything below describing it as a living city is SIP-only]

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** A zero-emissions city from its literal founding — wind and solar power, no fossil fuels, zero waste discharge, built into a site so exposed to katabatic winds (gusts to 300 km/h) that nothing about survival here was ever passive. Every watt and every degree of heat depended on continuously and actively balanced systems, with no natural fallback. A genuinely striking wind-turbine-and-solar-array skyline against extreme wind. Likely buried underground energy-storage/reserve infrastructure (flywheels, battery banks, thermal storage), built underground specifically because the surface was too wind-exposed to safely house redundant capacity above ground.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Constant, hands-on system-balancing and maintenance work — engineers actively managing power flow every day as a matter of survival, not convenience, closer to crewing a ship than running a static town. A demographic crossroads city — the only Halley subnet city with two co-Primary founding populations (American and, unusually, Japanese, via an eastern-approach immigration current) — giving it a genuinely blended, "meeting in the middle" daily social texture unlike its neighbors.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **Founders' Turbine** — commemorating the founding zero-emissions philosophy; observed through actual public maintenance/inspection of wind and solar infrastructure, treating upkeep itself as the ceremony.
- **The Crossing** (distinct holiday from Halley's own) — marking the city's dual American/Japanese demographic character, involving exchange of food, language, or custom between the two communities.
- Functioned informally as the last practical waypoint for pilgrims traveling toward Dome Fuji's Ice Cold Buddhism sites from the western Halley subnet — a rest-stop role, never a shrine of its own.

**Notable specific locations:** the wind/solar farms; the buried reserve/storage infrastructure and its (now severed) integration tunnels; the informal pilgrim waypoint.

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] A "keeping the lights on" crew drama — an entire episode following the literal, physical, minute-to-minute labor of balancing a zero-fallback power grid during a wind event, high tension purely from infrastructure. A pilgrim-waypoint episode, showing travelers passing through toward Dome Fuji.

**[POST-WAR — the destruction, and the ruins]:** Struck with precision at the connective integration points between surface generation and the underground reserve — not a broad strike, but one that understood exactly what would unmake the city's design. The reserve may well still sit underground today, intact and totally inaccessible, cut off rather than destroyed — a literal buried treasure/tomb under the ruins. A small straggling survivor population remains among the ruins, exposed once again to the same 300 km/h gales the original design existed to manage. Strongest possible "ghost town, but the ghosts are alive" episode setting: survivors camped in the shell of a city built entirely around active, continuous systems that no longer function, in a wind that never stopped.

### BELGRANO

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** "The airbase that never stood down" — a hard, disciplined, function-before-comfort Argentine Air Force aviation hub; runway inheritance made it the Halley subnet's primary aviation hub in its living era. Garage-and-mechanic culture is central and visible: genuine engine-repair shops, a place where "for anything with an engine, there's reliably somebody who can fix it." This mechanic culture also birthed a real garage-rock music scene (heavy engineering culture producing artistic innovation, not just coexisting alongside it).

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Air Force-structured civic and working life extended even to non-Air Force residents — chain-of-command social structure, maintenance rotations, near-martial civic bearing, carried forward as living institutional culture. Belgrano built Tepenia's first native overland vehicles ("las Arrastradoras," worn down over generations into the international "Rastra") and sent an all-robot expedition crew (using Belgrano's own archived flight charts, aerial survey photography, and meteorological data — filed as ordinary institutional records, not a self-conscious "archive") to discover the buried ruins of Byrd Station, with material support shipped from Palmer City and Rothera across the Weddell Sea. Garage rock scene and mechanic subculture are literally the same culture wearing two faces.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- Mate-sharing — an explicitly family/heritage practice, "one of the few places Belgrano allows itself something beyond pure function." A shared gourd passed hand to hand.
- **Bandera Day** — honoring Manuel Belgrano (the city's namesake) as creator of the Argentine flag, observed as civic-name pride rather than living ethnic heritage, generations after the founding Argentine population was demographically outpaced.
- **Expedition Day** — commemorating the Arrastradoras/Byrd-discovery crossing, the city's proudest historical chapter.

**Notable specific locations:** the airbase/runway; garage/mechanic workshops (great "found object" scavenging location — an old cross-DLC aircraft part sits forgotten in a scrap pile in one such garage); the archive of working flight charts and survey photography that later proved historically vital.

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] The Expedition Day send-off of the all-robot Byrd crossing crew — a founding-myth episode that could double as an origin story for an ageless robot character. A garage-rock gig episode blending mechanic culture and music culture. 

**[POST-WAR — "the Boneyard Times"]:** Belgrano survived the Long Night War intact, then *declined afterward*, slowly, into a stopped-mid-fall equilibrium its remaining residents call the Boneyard Times — not dead, but a dying city that stopped dying and just kept existing, run by people who inherited institutional discipline as habit rather than active service to an institution that no longer exists. A genuine, unresolved internal social split: "staying is loyalty" vs. "staying is just what happened to you" — and whether the nickname "Boneyarders" was ever chosen or just applied. The mate ritual is one of the only pieces of pre-war culture that plausibly crossed intact into this era (it was never institutional, so it didn't need the vanished institution to survive it). This is an unusually rich post-war "faded institution town" setting — think a naval base town after the base closes, people still doing maintenance rounds out of muscle memory. Strong setting for late-series/finale material if the show follows the timeline to 2812 and beyond, or for a flash-forward/epilogue.

### SANAY

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** Built on genuine bedrock (Vesleskarvet nunatak) rather than moving ice — endurance instead of exemption from war damage, later. Endless shipyards and a huge working port; industrialized and gritty; humans and robots working what looks like all hours, on a rotating freighter-arrival schedule rather than a fixed day/night rhythm — ship horns periodically piercing the atmosphere and echoing across the city. Truckyards and warehouses occupy the lower city; high rocky clifftops hold trucking/shipping depots and the import-export business district — and, woven into that same clifftop terrain (not in its own dedicated facility), the *actual* technical nerve center of the whole Halley subnet's communications network (the Arcanet relay nexus — even though the subnet is *named* for Halley, the real infrastructure sits here). Residential, commercial, and leisure districts run along the cliffside and surrounding expanse for off-shift workers.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Dockworking, shipyard labor, trucking/logistics dispatch, import-export business — a genuinely major shipping and transportation hub by function, not a quiet backwater. An informal, cross-crew "trading practical know-how off-shift" culture among dockworkers and truckers from different backgrounds — the working-port equivalent of engineers swapping ideas at a bar. A formally bounded port zone (proposed name: **the Trade Yard**) where incoming South African freighter crews dock, unload, and conduct business under defined protocols distinct from the rest of the port — a controlled contact zone with Upper Earth-adjacent shipping, welcoming but firmly bounded (echoing a Canton-System-style "trade yes, but on our terms" arrangement).

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:** SANAE research heritage survives mostly as institutional/ceremonial vocabulary and the **Founders' Bedrock** holiday, essentially invisible in what people actually do day to day (most residents' working hours have nothing to do with science). Civic identity explicitly named "Competence Without Commentary" — refusing to frame its own endurance as luck or specialness worth discussing.

**Notable specific locations:** the shipyards/dockside; the clifftop business district and Arcanet nexus; the Trade Yard (bounded foreign-freighter zone); the residential cliffside districts (repaired later/more slowly than the port after the war, per Full Extrapolation — a visible class-inflected texture where the workplace was restored before the neighborhoods were).

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] A dockside procedural — freighter arrival, unloading, dispatch — as a structural spine for an episode juggling multiple characters' storylines simultaneously (busy, loud, functional setting). A "Trade Yard" episode centered on a tense, bounded, transactional encounter with Upper Earth-adjacent visitors — different in flavor from more open contact elsewhere in Tepenia. [POST-WAR] Rebuilding-priority tension: the port was fixed first because it had to keep functioning, and the neighborhoods where people actually live took much longer — a quietly resentment-adjacent inequality that cuts across national/robot-human lines and instead tracks purely by "do you work the docks or not."

### LAZAR — Tepenia's single largest city (both censuses)

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** A genuine highrise megacity — tall buildings in every direction, holographic advertisements and multicolored lights filling the air, constant movement of people to and from work — yet quieter than its scale suggests thanks to a layered transit network (maglev primary, electric secondary, elevated tertiary). The only Halley subnet city with no single founding population: two separate settlements (Novolazarevskaya and the unoccupied Maitri site) grew together over roughly a century during the First Interwar Period until the seam between them stopped mattering — a real "Twin Cities"/Budapest-style coalescence, not a metaphor. The city still legibly splits into two "moods": the old Novolazarevskaya core (quieter, more institutional, more reserved, "never gone dark" civic pride) and the former-Maitri expansion (the bulk of the holographic-advertisement commercial density, faster and more overflow-driven energy) — crossing from one district to the other is meant to feel like a mood shift within the same city, not a hard border.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** A genuine commercial/logistics capital — Lazar sits at the convergence of Hwy 7 proper and the Belgrano Highway Extension, the only point in the subnet where both routes meet, and its sheer population (1.8 million) generates enormous internal consumer demand on its own. ~20% of its economy is engineering/technical work, a direct descendant of Novolazarevskaya's research tradition, with an ongoing professional relationship to Neumayer's precision-engineering culture nearby. A dense, always-on, commuter-heavy megacity daily rhythm — the closest thing this project offers to a "real modern city" backdrop within Antarctica.

**Cultural practices and rituals [UNDATED]:** The demonym "Lazarian" carries an unplanned but locally noticed echo of "Lazarus" — residents plausibly comment on this themselves, given the city's own survival story (badly bombed, not destroyed, purely because of its own scale) inverts the Biblical story: nothing external saved Lazar, its own accumulated size did. Good material for local folklore/urban legend texture, or a running verbal motif.

**Notable specific locations:** the Novolazarevskaya "old core" district; the former-Maitri commercial-density expansion district; the Hwy 7 / Belgrano Highway Extension junction.

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] A pure "big city" episode — density, neon, crowds, commuter transit — a tonal palette break from the rest of the subnet's harsher/quieter settlements, useful for contrast. A two-district episode literally staged as a journey from the old core to the expansion (or vice versa) to dramatize a generational or tonal contrast within one city. [POST-WAR] Per the GDD's own flagged finding, Lazar's most visually striking, commercially vibrant district (the former-Maitri expansion) is *also* its most war-scarred one — holographic advertisements glowing over visibly patched, rebuilt, or still-scarred structures, vitality and damage sharing the same skyline rather than being sorted into separate "pretty" and "ruined" zones. This is flagged in the GDD itself as the single most evocative image the research produced — strongly recommended as a location reference image/mood board item.

### ABOWASA — Tepenia's only jointly-founded city

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** Small, intimate, household-and-neighborhood scale rather than institutional scale — Tepenia's only dual-national founding (Finnish and Swedish exile communities, from two real stations built only ~200 meters apart, meaning the two populations never had the option of staying separate). Sits on genuine mainland bedrock (Basen nunatak) — stable ground, unlike neighboring Halley and Neumayer. A civic culture organized around intimacy: "everyone knows everyone," Baarle-Hertog/Sark/Whittier-style governance at near-household scale.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** A real research/technical economy (25% of industry, the largest single sector) — inherited Finnish/Swedish (FINNARP/SWEDARP) research tradition, with a specific developed niche: geological and structural-stability research, i.e. expertise in building on solid rock versus unstable ice, directly useful (and plausibly quietly exported/consulted) to neighboring Halley and Neumayer, both of which are still solving the exact problem Abowasa's own bedrock made moot generations ago. Daily material culture — cuisine, fashion, craft, music — genuinely hybridized between Finnish and Swedish influence (a small community never had separate kitchens or wardrobes to maintain); language and civic memory, by contrast, stayed distinct — Finnish and Swedish both survive as living household languages, neither displacing the other. New arrivals are described as making a fast, near-inevitable transition from "visitor" to "resident."

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **Turku Remembrance** — a specifically Finnish-heritage observance (Turku being Finland's oldest city/historical capital), kept alive as cultural memory rather than daily practice.
- **The Joint Founding** — a shared Finnish-Swedish civic holiday marking the original dual settlement, belonging to neither nation alone — likely the more communally significant of the two, since it celebrates the fact that makes Abowasa unique in all of Tepenia.
- Both real founding stations were built for year-round occupation but staffed only seasonally by deliberate budgetary choice, not necessity — a recurring civic instinct toward comfortable, deliberate choice over grinding through hardship (a genuinely distinct emotional register from most of this subnet's harder-won survival stories).

**Notable specific locations:** the bedrock foundation itself as a visible, tangible point of local pride/identity (solid ground, unlike neighbors); the household-scale civic gathering spaces implied by its intimate governance style.

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] A "small town where everyone knows everyone" episode, contrasting sharply with Lazar's anonymity or Sanay's industrial grind — good for an intimate, low-stakes-but-emotionally-dense entry focused on relationships rather than infrastructure. A newcomer's fast integration into the community as a structural device for introducing an audience-surrogate character. [POST-WAR] Damage pattern specifically targeted the labs/research infrastructure while sparing the household/neighborhood fabric — meaning post-war Abowasa can visually read as "a small, warm, still-functioning residential town sitting next to visibly ruined lab buildings and instrument arrays" — technical heart hit, human (and robot) heart mostly intact. A strong, legible post-war visual contrast for a single scene or location.

## MAWSON SUBNET

Real-world basis: Mac.Robertson Land, Prince Harald Coast, and the East Antarctic Plateau interior. This is the smallest of Tepenia's subnets (3 cities) and, per the GDD's own explicit finding, the *least internally connected* — each of its three cities has a more developed relationship with a neighboring subnet (Halley or Mirny) than with its own subnet-mates. For the show, this means: don't treat "Mawson subnet" as a tight regional unit the way Halley subnet is. Treat these three cities as strikingly different registers that happen to share an administrative label — useful for a season structured around discontinuity/journey rather than one contiguous community.

---

### MAWSON (the city, subnet hub)

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** The oldest continuously-occupied site in its subnet (Australian-founded, 1954 real-world station, unbroken occupation) — but the defining, deliberately surprising civic fact is that Mawson functions as close to a resort town as Antarctica can produce: genuine warm hospitality, public libraries, real ease of making friends, and status as **Tepenia's go-to honeymoon destination** for newly-married human-robot couples — a reputation built deliberately over decades, not stumbled into (explicit real-world parallel: Jeju City's real "Honeymoon Island" branding). A real share of visitors aren't here on hub/logistics business at all — they're couples on a one-to-two-week honeymoon before returning home to build lives elsewhere.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Hospitality-sector work at real scale — hoteliers, guides, hosts — alongside the ordinary hub-city logistics of sitting on Hwy 4 between the Sayowa Junction and Soyuz. A customary honeymoon activity: a visit to the Prince Charles Mountains overlook above Horseshoe Harbour, timed to whichever extreme is in season (polar night or midnight sun) — couples make the trip regardless of which one they get, and treat it as their defining shared memory of the trip. A demographic mix (Chinese-Primary, with Japanese, South Korean, Indonesian, and founding-wave Australian communities all holding real Significant-tier presence) that the GDD explicitly proposes as the *cause*, not just a coincidence alongside, the city's hospitality culture — genuine plurality meaning no single group can gatekeep what "being a local" means, producing an Odessa-style "legendary hospitality" as a practical civic default.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:** "The Name That Outlasted the Founders" — the Australian founding-nation name/legacy is honored with complete, unbroken institutional weight (echoes of St. Douglas veneration, the old Australian Antarctic Territory claim, now purely ceremonial/historical post-Split-Brain) even though the lived population is now Chinese-Primary — and, distinctively, the city has made *open, accepted peace* with that gap rather than suppressing or dramatizing it (a deliberate contrast the GDD draws against Jeju City's real suppressed history — Mawson's fault line is openly acknowledged, not buried).

**Notable specific locations:** the honeymoon-hospitality infrastructure (hotels, guided-tour circuits); the Prince Charles Mountains overlook above Horseshoe Harbour (a specific, nameable romantic-vista location); public libraries as a notable, deliberately warm civic amenity.

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] A wedding/honeymoon-arc episode — genuinely warm, low-conflict, character-focused, usable as a tonal break or as the setting for a robot-human marriage plotline given the city's specific cultural role. A "hospitality worker's-eye view" episode following the staff who host generation after generation of honeymooning couples, giving an ageless robot character a unique vantage point on decades of other people's happiest days. [POST-WAR] The hub's *outward-facing* Arcanet link (to the rest of Tepenia) was severed by the war while its *internal* links to Sayowa and Dome Fuji plausibly held — meaning post-war Mawson could still function as an intact, working "island" whose isolation is informational/infrastructural rather than physical.

### SAYOWA — "The Point Where Three Roads Meet"

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP]:** A small, modest city (one of the smallest in its subnet) sitting exactly where the Mawson subnet, the inland route to Concordia, and the Halley subnet physically converge. Splits cleanly into two visual/functional halves: industrialized fabrication and trucking/dispatch infrastructure on one side, genuine residential neighborhoods on the other — leisure culture present but visibly secondary, tucked into the residential half rather than commanding civic space. On East Ongul Island; connection to the mainland genuinely changes by season — a winter ice road usable once sea ice thickens enough for vehicle traffic, maritime/icebreaker access only in summer thaw. A major fabrication industry paired with a huge trucking/dispatch operation; plausibly "the whole continent's connectivity" depends in part on the junction this small city occupies.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** Freight consolidation, trucking dispatch, fabrication/manufacturing work — a genuine logistics-and-production town whose importance has nothing to do with its own modest population. The name itself is a phonetic descendant of "Shōwa" (the Japanese imperial era, 1926–1989, spanning war, defeat, reconstruction) folded into a small Antarctic exile city's proper noun — for the demographic majority (China-Primary) it's just an ordinary place-name; for the smaller Japanese-heritage community specifically, it still carries real, felt generational weight, discussed privately rather than performed publicly.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:** Quiet, dutiful, function-over-recognition civic character ("small vessel, outsized cargo" is the GDD's own summary phrase) — the junction itself is maintained through informal cooperative arrangement among the parties who depend on it (Sayowa, Mawson, Concordia-route and Halley-subnet interests) rather than by open contest — nobody has fully claimed it because everyone who matters needs it kept running. A meaningfully different texture from Troll's openly-contested airfield in the neighboring subnet: cooperative fragility rather than open conflict.

**Notable specific locations:** the fabrication/dispatch half of the city; the residential half; the seasonal ice road / icebreaker crossing to the mainland — a strong, literally seasonal set-piece (a road that only exists part of the year).

**Strong episode settings:** [SIP] An ice-road episode — the tension and physical specificity of a seasonal, temporary overland crossing, good for a journey/thriller structure or a supply-run plot. A quiet "the people who keep everything running and get no credit for it" ensemble episode centered on dispatch workers. [POST-WAR] Sayowa was damaged but deliberately *not* fully destroyed — spared specifically because an attacker's own supply needs depended on keeping the junction functional (a genuinely distinct war-damage logic within the GDD's own framework: "strategic value preserved by restraint," not luck, not bedrock, not scale). Post-war Sayowa should read as damaged-but-deliberately-kept-alive, a different visual note from Troll's openly fought-over ruin.

### DOME FUJI — a research station that became a robot monastery

**Physical environment / architecture [SIP/UNDATED — this city's whole "present" as described is essentially timeless within the SIP, since its transition from station to monastery predates the war and it survives the war unchanged]:** One of the highest points in Tepenia by real elevation (~3,810m geometric, ~4,500m effective physiological altitude) — but it does not look like a summit. Total, almost imperceptible flatness in every direction; no ridge, no shadow, no visual drama. At depth of polar night: −65°C, near-total stillness (4–6 m/s wind), diamond dust hanging in the air catching what little light there is into halos and sun-dogs, and an aurora australis that residents insist visibly *slows* here — understood doctrinally as the aurora "listening." This is a 100%-robot population (55,072 at last census) — no humans present, and human presence is formally forbidden (an altitude/cold lethality rule, explicitly not hostility). The population arrived one devotee at a time, across decades, drawn from roughly 34 different Tepenian cities by religious conviction alone (Ice Cold Buddhism) — not nationality, not ancestry, not economic necessity. It is architecturally a repurposed JARE ice-core research station (**the Old Wing** — labs, drilling equipment, researcher housing, now living/maintenance space, "a research station's bones doing a monastery's work"), plus explicitly religious additions: **Nyakkyo** ("the Still Point") — a small, low, deliberately unremarkable and unheated structure built at the dome's exact (surveyed, not visually obvious) geometric high point, containing no altar or icon, just the coldest, stillest room the faith can arrange, where devotees sit alone; **the Robing Hall**, a transitional space where devotees change into white robes before the final approach to Nyakkyo; **the Archive Vault**, holding ice-core samples spanning hundreds of thousands of years of climate record, kept at genuinely extreme cold; and **the Waystones**, low unremarkable cairns marking the final stretch of the dangerous interior plateau crossing pilgrims must complete to arrive at all — turning back past the last Waystone is considered to carry real spiritual weight.

**Daily activities and occupations [SIP]:** A quiet devotional life organized around presence and stillness rather than labor, though the Old Wing's maintenance and the Archive Vault's light-touch preservation work (tended by a handful of devotees who arrived with prior scientific training) continue as secondary vocations, not abandoned entirely. Wearing white robes specifically to physically commune with the cold — possible because robot bodies don't suffer frostbite/hypothermia the way humans would; doctrine treats *not* using that capacity fully (over-insulating, avoiding real cold contact) as a quiet failure of devotion, akin to a monk declining an achievable fast.

**Cultural practices and rituals [SIP]:**
- **Deepest Cold** — the closest thing to a universal mandatory communal event: falls at the precise coldest point of polar night (tracked with genuine meteorological seriousness, can shift by days year to year); every devotee physically present attends; pilgrims who happen to complete their crossing near this date are considered to have arrived under unusually significant circumstances.
- **The Crossing** — individually observed on the anniversary of each devotee's own arrival, marked with a partial re-enactment: spending part of the day outside in the robes, away from heated structures, for a self-set duration that devotees extend slightly year over year — an informal, undramatic marker of standing within the community (never described as hierarchy, but functions like one).
- No single named leader; a rotating, informal group of longest-standing devotees (those who've observed the most Deepest Colds) function as de facto stewards of Nyakkyo and the ritual calendar, by simple accumulated tenure rather than appointment.
- The faith holds two primary sacred sites in a doctrinally *complementary, not competing* relationship: Dome Fuji ("arriving" — devotion through pure presence, universal draw, communing with cold via stillness) and Kunlun in Mirny subnet ("reaching" — devotion through vocation/construction, curated draw, communing with cold via active work aimed at the sky/orbit). A small number of devotees make the pilgrimage between both sites over a long devotional life, informally regarded as a mark of unusually complete devotion.

**Notable specific locations:** Nyakkyo; the Robing Hall; the Archive Vault; the Waystones and the final stretch of plateau crossing; the Old Wing.

**Strong episode settings:** A pilgrimage-journey episode — the dangerous, visually stark interior plateau crossing itself, ending at Nyakkyo, is a self-contained, powerful stand-alone structure (arrival, stillness, revelation). A Deepest Cold communal-observance episode as a season-culminating ensemble gathering. Because every devotee is (per the GDD's own cross-reference finding) essentially a "time capsule" of their home city frozen at the moment they left — no live Arcanet feed, word-of-mouth only — Dome Fuji is a uniquely strong site for a *retrospective/clue-gathering* episode: a character seeking outdated-but-real information about a distant, possibly now-destroyed city could only get it here, from someone who simply hasn't heard news in years. Note also the GDD's own strongest unresolved idea (Finding 7): a possible ancient caretaker-robot who never left after the original human research station emptied out, centuries before the current devotee population arrived — the only individual at Dome Fuji with lived (non-doctrinal) memory of humans, and by the numbers, plausibly one of very few residents anywhere in Tepenia old enough to remember both a secular researcher's daily life and a monastery's. This is an extremely strong hook for one of Southern Lights' ageless robot-lead characters: someone who could plausibly have been physically present at Dome Fuji as this exact figure, watching a research station complete its slow, undramatic emptying over a century, then watching an entirely different population arrive generations later and make the place sacred.

**[POST-WAR]:** Undamaged — Dome Fuji's isolation and lack of strategic value protected it outright; nothing here needs a "ruins" register at all, making it one of the very few Halley/Mawson-subnet locations usable as a stable, unchanged setting across the entire 248-year span without needing separate SIP/post-war treatments.

## CROSS-CITY AND CROSS-SUBNET RELATIONSHIPS (for continuity/travel/rivalry planning)

**The Halley subnet supply chain (a literal, trackable freight/travel route) [SIP]:** Sanay's port receives incoming shipments → goods split, trucked either directly out via Hwy 7 to nearby cities, or trucked onward to Troll → Troll's airfield flies goods to the rest of Tepenia. This creates real mutual dependency: Troll has nothing to fly without Sanay's freight; Sanay can't reach remote destinations without Troll's runway. Halley, having deliberately built no docks or airstrip of its own, depends entirely on this chain via Belgrano and Sanay — meaning Halley's day-to-day wellbeing (does it eat this month) is a downstream, visible readout of a conflict happening entirely at Troll, a city Halley has no control over. **This is a ready-made, physically legible three-city storyline spine**: a supply run, a courier, or a family with members in more than one of these three cities would let the show dramatize the chain directly.

**The Rastra / Byrd Station throughway (a founding-era road-trip/heist-adjacent storyline, good material for flashback or origin-story episodes) [SIP]:** Belgrano's crews built a crude first-generation overland hauler but needed it redesigned for a long-distance crossing; a small crew drove it to Neumayer, whose founding-era engineers (in Neumayer's very first years, before the city had any reputation to protect) reverse-engineered and rebuilt it into the vehicle that became the "Rastra" — the vehicle template every Tepenian still drives generations later. That rebuilt vehicle is what an all-robot Belgrano crew then drove out to discover the buried ruins of Byrd Station, using Belgrano's own archived survey maps and material support shipped in from Palmer City and Rothera (a different subnet entirely). Present-day civic memory of this event is genuinely split and non-competing: Belgrano loudly, proudly claims the Rastra as its own founding achievement; Neumayer, true to its quiet, precision-first character, never publicly claims it at all — it's simply in the private engineering archive, as complete and exact as everything else Neumayer keeps. **A single object (the Rastra/vehicle) or a single expedition could plausibly connect an ageless robot character to at least four different cities across two subnets over centuries** — Belgrano, Neumayer, Palmer City, Rothera — a strong throughline for a robot lead's backstory.

**Abowasa–Halley–Neumayer "ground stability triangle" [SIP]:** Halley (ski-mounted, always relocating) and Neumayer (hydraulic legs) both live on unstable ice and have built elaborate active engineering to cope; Abowasa sits on solid bedrock and has developed real geological/structural-stability research as an economic niche directly relevant to both neighbors. A plausible, not-yet-dramatized consulting/trade relationship exists here — three neighboring cities effectively running a natural experiment in how to build on Antarctic ground.

**Halley subnet's overall civic register [SIP]:** a recurring "does the real work, doesn't take the credit" trait shows up independently in at least five of the eight cities (Halley/Sanay's nexus arrangement, Neumayer's uncredited engineering legacy, Belgrano's modest garage/archive habits, Sanay's "Competence Without Commentary," Troll's understated prosperity, Abowasa's low-key competence). If Southern Lights wants a subnet-wide *feel* distinct from flashier regions of Tepenia, this is it: capable people who plainly do not need or expect applause.

**Mawson subnet's relative disconnection [SIP → POST-WAR]:** Unlike Halley subnet, Mawson subnet's three cities are not tightly bound to each other — each has a stronger relationship outward. Sayowa bridges toward Halley subnet (specifically toward Princess Elisabeth, the two are the most geographically adjacent inter-subnet cities in all of Tepenia, with plausible generations-old informal contact via Japanese-heritage families on both sides, predating any formal highway link). Dome Fuji's real community is Kunlun, in the separate Mirny subnet — a religious/emotional tie that matters more than anything within Mawson subnet itself; Dome Fuji's historical aviation supply route ran through Troll (Halley subnet) and an unnamed Korean city (Mirny subnet), not through Mawson or Sayowa. Mawson itself sits on the highway toward the same Mirny subnet cluster. **Practical implication for the show:** don't force a "Mawson subnet community" episode structure — these three cities work better as three genuinely disconnected registers (resort warmth, workaday junction, monastic isolation) that a traveling character passes through, each one feeling like arriving somewhere else entirely, rather than as a cohesive regional ensemble.

**Sequencing note for the writers' room:** given SIP spans 2564–2812, almost everything tagged [SIP] above is available for any point in the show's core run. Everything tagged [POST-WAR] only becomes true in the last moments of the timeline (2812 onward) — appropriate for a series finale, an epilogue/flash-forward, or (if the show wants a framing device) a present-day wraparound narrated from the post-war vantage point looking back at the 248 years of SIP material above.

## JANBOGO SUBNET (Ross Sea Region)

Real-world basis: the Ross Sea coast — Ross Island/Hut Point Peninsula, Cape Adare, Terra Nova Bay, Cape Denison, and the Adélie Land coast. Seven cities: Janbogo, Zukelli, Fort McMurdo, Cape Adare, Denison, Dumont d'Urville, Scott. Connected by Hwy 183 ("the Janbogo Highway").

**War-status overview for the subnet** (all destruction dated to 2812, i.e., right at your show's end point):
- **Survived, damaged:** Janbogo, Fort McMurdo, Scott, Dumont d'Urville
- **Destroyed [POST-WAR]:** Zukelli, Cape Adare, Denison

This means for a 248-year show, Zukelli, Cape Adare, and Denison are **living, thriving cities for virtually the entire runtime** — they only become ruins in the show's final year/episodes. That's dramatically significant: you could build deep multi-episode/multi-season attachment to Zukelli or Cape Adare specifically because the audience knows (or eventually learns) they don't survive.

---

### JANBOGO — "the teahouse-and-shielded-mall city, witness to Zukelli's destruction"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:**
Coastal city on Terra Nova Bay (~74°37'S), built on Jang Bogo Station infrastructure. Defined by katabatic winds off the East Antarctic interior — not episodic storms but a near-permanent condition; wind chill, not ambient temperature, is what people plan around. The Terra Nova Bay polynya keeps a stretch of water ice-free year-round — a genuine strategic/maritime asset, the only Ross Sea city with true year-round sea access (real-world parallel: Murmansk). Streets and interiors are richly colored and heavily insulated — a fusion of Chinese and French design sensibility. The city's real architectural signature is its **vast shielded commercial halls** — brightly lit, bustling indoor civic space (direct real-world parallel: Dubai mall culture, but inverted for cold instead of heat) where much of daily life happens while the exterior stays dark, windswept, and nearly empty.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:**
Shopping, socializing, and working inside the great shielded halls. Teahouse culture as *the* defining civic activity — Majyao Bisyugota's establishment (later relocated to Concordia) was famous throughout Tepenia, modeled on Central Asian chaykhana and Ottoman kahvehane traditions: tea as hospitality infrastructure, turning down an offered tea considered close to a social offense, teahouses functioning as hubs for storytelling, business dealing, and news exchange. Port and highway logistics work (Janbogo is the region's primary coastal supply/transit hub). Katabatic wind-forecasting (a formalized local specialty — outdoor fatalities are rare specifically because of this tradition).

**[SIP] Cultural practices / social texture:**
A city defined by *absorbing* whatever reaches it and making it "Janbogo's own" — Chinese-French fashion fusion, a teahouse tradition that "traveled well beyond its Korean roots" the way New Orleans jazz became the whole city's sound regardless of origin. Genuinely diverse population (China/USA co-Primary, but South Korea only Significant despite the city's "Korean" reputation) — good texture for an ensemble show: no single dominant culture, lots of code-switching and blending.

**Notable locations:** Majyao's original teahouse (a preserved site of civic memory even after her departure); the great shielded commercial halls; the waterfront (from which Zukelli's ruins are visible on clear days — **this framing only exists after 2812**, so pre-war the waterfront is simply Janbogo's civic/harbor front, not yet a memorial vista).

**[POST-WAR] material (useful mainly for a finale/epilogue, not ongoing SIP scenes):** Janbogo damaged but the most functionally critical surviving coastal link to Concordia; residents split between a minority who keep an unofficial "vigil" at the waterfront on personal anniversaries, and a majority who've made peace with not looking at the Zukelli ruins — real, quiet civic tension between the two groups, not a resolved consensus. Good material if the show wants to depict the immediate aftermath of the war in its final episodes.

**Strong episode settings:** A teahouse scene as ensemble hangout/information-hub (the "everyone passes through here eventually" location); the shielded commercial hall as a bustling social set-piece (market day, festival, a chase or confrontation staged against crowds); Hwy 183 supply convoys arriving/departing as a recurring visual rhythm tying episodes together.

### ZUKELLI — "Janbogo's Italian sister city, destroyed near its own peak" — the show's most emotionally loaded SIP-only location

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:**
~8km north of Janbogo, same bay, same polynya, same katabatic winds. Explicitly **tangled, organically-grown, built as needs arose rather than to any master plan** — genuinely disorienting to outsiders, second nature to residents (real-world parallels: Valparaíso's 42 hillside "cerros," Venice's organically-fused islands, Kraków's Kazimierz district). Elaborate plantlines and elevated "bridger-footroads" thread between buildings — an artificial, engineered approximation of equatorial greenery, a warm/green visual contrast against the Antarctic dark just outside the heating envelope. A genuine, informal wayfinding system reportedly existed (color/pattern-marked heating-pipe conduits, neighborhood by neighborhood, learned only by living there — never a formal signage system).

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:**
Food and hospitality are the absolute center of civic life (Italian-rooted, exported subnet-wide — "people traveled from Janbogo specifically for it"). A huge, genre-diverse music scene performed mostly in restaurants with their own dedicated performance stages rather than standalone venues — music and food as adjacent expressions of the same hospitality instinct (think: dinner-and-a-show as the default night out, everywhere, informally).

**Cultural practices / social texture:** Described repeatedly and specifically as a place with genuine *soul* — destroyed near its own demographic peak (72.5% human retention at time of destruction), not in decline. Italy's cultural presence concentrated here the way Korea's is in Janbogo, but never got the multi-generational "settling in" Janbogo's culture had — it was cut off mid-vitality. An established "Crossing" tradition of informal, casual visiting back and forth between Zukelli and Janbogo (blurring who was hosting whom) — great texture for cross-city relationships, romances, family ties in your ensemble.

**Notable locations:** Elisa Faranda's restaurant-and-performance-space (proposed as Zukelli's single most famous venue, recognized even in Janbogo); the tangled unnamed residential quarters navigable only by locals.

**[POST-WAR, DO NOT USE AS SIP]** Destroyed 2812 as a **deliberate, punitive, total-kill strike** — Zukelli sheltered a small multi-national group (proposed 4–7 people) of AWOL Upper Earth defectors, and was destroyed specifically to send an inescapable deterrent message to the rest of Tepenia; Janbogo was spared specifically to witness and relay the message. No advance warning was possible — total surprise was structurally necessary to the strike's purpose. This is a **series-culminating event**, not backstory — since Zukelli sat at "near its own demographic peak" throughout the SIP, the show can spend enormous time there as a living, beloved place before this happens.

**Strong episode settings:** A season (or entire early-season arc) built around Zukelli as the "warm, chaotic, delicious, musical" counterpoint city to Janbogo's more orderly civic warmth — sister-city rivalry/romance material, food-and-music set pieces, the tangled streets as a recurring "you have to know someone" navigation device for a newcomer character. Held in reserve: the destruction itself as a devastating structural event near the show's endpoint, watched in real time from Janbogo's waterfront by characters who cannot help.

### FORT MCMURDO — "the historical capital, presence not population, lives in an active volcano's shadow"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:**
Built on McMurdo Station's infrastructure (Ross Island), the largest built environment in Tepenia without being the most populous city. **Mount Erebus dominates every skyline view** — the southernmost active volcano on Earth, a permanent lava lake, a constant gas/ash plume on the horizon in most weather (activity level proposed as stable/baseline, not heightened — atmospheric backdrop, not an ongoing crisis). Working-class, direct, meritocratic culture — status earned by capability, not origin, with transient/seasonal contract labor a founding-era staple of the mining/extraction economy. Dedicated comms stations physically coordinate supply, extraction scheduling, inter-city logistics — a felt, visible "real business happening here" quality distinct from Janbogo's more social hub role.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Fuel processing, equipment manufacture/repair, construction-materials production (Fort McMurdo itself as processing/coordination hub; raw extraction happens across McMurdo Sound in the Dry Valleys' exposed bedrock — a genuine two-site economy, akin to Calgary/oil-sands). Volcanology and Erebus-monitoring as an ongoing, generations-deep institutional tradition — familiarity with living beside an active volcano, "clarifying" rather than unsettling to residents. Coordinating supply/logistics decisions that "ripple outward" and shape what the rest of the Federation can do — this is Tepenia's historical seat of soft power, informal at first, formally named capital later (proposed: after the Amundsen Tower's construction, ~2629–2639).

**Cultural practices:** Blunt, unglamorous, competence-first culture with zero interest in performing importance — "presence, not population." A genuine political undercurrent worth dramatizing: the "McMurdo Problem" — resentment from other cities about the city's outsized *informal* influence, eventually forcing the capital status to become official and accountable.

**Notable locations:** Cape Evans and Cape Royds — the Heroic Age expedition huts, sitting a few kilometers from the industrial city, preserved almost perfectly (a genuine time-capsule/contrast location, "the Heroic Age preserved beside its industrial-age descendant"); the harbor/pier/fuel-depot infrastructure (heaviest, oldest, most overbuilt part of the city).

**Strong episode settings:** A "seat of government" episode — city council/national-decision drama physically staged with Erebus smoking in every establishing shot; a contrast scene at Cape Evans/Cape Royds (quiet historical pilgrimage vs. the industrial churn a few km away); labor/contract-worker storylines (transient seasonal workforce = built-in mechanism for characters passing through).

### CAPE ADARE — "where Antarctica's human story began; a huge city that feels like a small town"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Geographically vast but low-density — "looks big on a map, feels like a small town on the ground" (achieved at real scale: Census I population over 1.49M, larger than Janbogo). Heating infrastructure creates a genuine warm microclimate, "close to an oasis by Antarctic standards." Built around **Borchgrevink's 1899 hut**, literally the first human structure on the Antarctic continent — the whole city grew up around and with this heritage site.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Life moves slowly and deliberately — "people and robots work, but nobody rushes." Acoustic folk music culture (guitars, violins, cellos, tagelharpas — American folk-instrument subculture, not Norwegian despite the Scandinavian instruments) as the city's default texture of leisure. Keeping individual penguins as outdoor pets (uncommon but not unheard of) alongside one of the largest Adélie rookeries in the world (250,000+ breeding pairs) — smell, noise, and constant penguin presence is simply part of daily background life, "neighbors, not tourists" (direct real-world parallel: Boulders Beach/Simon's Town, South Africa).

**Cultural practices:** A civic identity organized entirely around **firstness** — the oldest, most historically-descended city, not the largest or wealthiest. Proposed civic observance "St. Carsten's Landing" (Feb 17, the historical Southern Cross expedition's real landfall date) — a plausible anchor for a recurring seasonal festival episode. A genuinely mixed founding population with no single dominant nation (chance-based: whoever's ships happened to make landfall first, since this is the most accessible Ross Sea landfall point from open ocean) — good texture for a true melting-pot ensemble location.

**Notable locations:** Borchgrevink's hut itself (the literal "first building"); the rookery, functionally a co-inhabited neighborhood, not a nature preserve set apart from the city.

**Strong episode settings:** A slow, pastoral, "small town" episode explicitly contrasted against the show's more urban/industrial locations — good for a season's tonal breather. A civic-holiday episode built around St. Carsten's Landing. Because Cape Adare is destroyed only in 2812 (late in the war, proposed as a *symbolic* rather than strategic target — striking "the place where Antarctica's human story began"), it's available as a living location for virtually the entire show and its eventual loss can land as a gut-punch precisely because it never felt militarily significant.

### DENISON — "the windiest inhabited place on Earth, built as one continuous fused structure"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Directly on Hwy 183 itself (a chokepoint, not a spur). Among the windiest permanent habitation sites at sea level on Earth — average winds ~80 km/h, gusts recorded over 300 km/h, near-constant rather than episodic. The **entire city is one continuous, aerodynamically coherent, load-sharing structure** — not occasional covered walkways (like Zukelli/Janbogo) but comprehensive structural interlinking throughout, so that moving through Denison means moving through one fused building (real-world parallels: Kowloon Walled City's fused-organism density, Montreal's RÉSO underground city, Trieste's Bora-wind-adapted chains/handrails/weighted roofs).

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Historically a manufacturer of fabrication-synthesis chambers (the "Cradle" robot-creation infrastructure) for the whole nation — genuinely load-bearing national industry, not just a curiosity. A distinctive, loud, proud verbal culture: residents recite Denison's own wind statistics to outsiders as something close to social ritual — the explicit *opposite* of the "quiet competence" ethos most Tepenian cities share. (The architecture itself is withdrawn/inward-sealed and gives no visible clue to outsiders how extreme conditions are — so the verbal boasting exists specifically to compensate for architecture that hides the city's own severity.)

**Notable locations:** No single landmark beyond the fused structure itself — "a handful of landmark structures remain distinctive and recognizable even at real distance through blowing snow."

**Strong episode settings:** An entire episode that plays with the "one building, no outside" conceit — characters navigating Denison entirely indoors, extreme wind as constant sound design/ambient threat just past the walls; a "loud pride" culture-clash scene (Denison characters instinctively reciting wind stats to visitors, baffling outsiders). Destroyed 2812 as a strategic highway-chokepoint-plus-industrial target (not punitive) — a good candidate for a season-ending "the war reaches the supply road" episode, distinct in tone/motive from Zukelli's punitive destruction.

### DUMONT D'URVILLE — "the francophone island city negotiating with wind and penguins, never fighting them"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Small island city (Petrel Island) at a genuine two-highway junction (Hwy 2's eastern terminus meets Hwy 183's northern terminus). Spatially stratified: dense, expensive, "genuinely alive" downtown core on the island itself, with heating units run *between* buildings as a visible outward display of vitality; cheaper residential territory across a mainland bridge (explicitly, deliberately left undecided in the GDD as either a permanent structure or a seasonal ice/boat crossing — either works for the show). Co-inhabited with one of Antarctica's largest Adélie penguin breeding grounds — the city "founded itself in the middle of an environment it would never fully own," not in opposition to it.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Live music in almost every eatery, described directly as **"New Orleans scaled down to 1/20th the size"** — many small, packed, informal venues rather than one big performance industry. A robot naturalist-observation tradition around the penguin colonies — described as "the closest thing the city has to a shared contemplative practice" (quiet, careful colony-watching as something close to communal meditation). St. Jules Day (Jan 21, commemorating Jules Dumont d'Urville's 1840 landing) proposed as an outdoor public reading of expedition journal fragments, held in the wind on purpose, regardless of conditions — an act of standing in the same conditions the original landing was made under.

**Cultural practices:** French remains the living civic default language despite a China/USA demographic majority arriving generations later (direct real-world parallel: Quebec City, 94% French-speaking after 400 years under other rule) — transmitted through ordinary daily civic life, not ceremony, so anyone living there for years picks up functional French regardless of ancestry. Robots meaningfully outnumber humans (island cap population, the wind keeps human population thin).

**Notable locations:** The downtown eatery-and-music strip; the mainland bridge itself as a visual/spatial anchor; the penguin colonies as an everyday co-inhabited space, not a nature reserve.

**Strong episode settings:** A dense, festive "small New Orleans" ensemble hangout episode — perfect recurring location for musicians/performers among your cast. A francophone-culture-in-an-anglophone-nation storyline (code-switching, language-as-identity for a character). The bridge itself as a location for a suspenseful crossing scene (aerodynamic flutter risk / pack ice loads are explicitly flagged as real engineering hazards in the GDD). St. Jules Day as a recurring seasonal festival beat.

### SCOTT — "the quiet, family-oriented neighbor of Fort McMurdo"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** ~3km from Fort McMurdo on the same peninsula, deliberately kept small by choice rather than necessity. Overwhelmingly residential in footprint — housing dominates, with casual restaurants/leisure spaces and a modest handful of public gathering spaces; the direct experiential opposite of Fort McMurdo's constant operational churn next door. At the civic heart: **Hut Point**, Robert Falcon Scott's 1902 expedition refuge, one of the oldest surviving human-made structures in Antarctica.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** A genuine, quiet technical/research economic base underneath the domestic surface — precision equipment calibration, maintenance, and environmental/geological monitoring, including a real, concrete shared Erebus-monitoring collaboration with Fort McMurdo (heavy geological/seismic work at Fort McMurdo, precision instrumentation at Scott) — a good "these two cities actually need each other" throughline. Also: collecting volcanic material fallen from Erebus for delivery to Fort McMurdo's processing.

**Cultural practices:** **On Tepenian Independence Day (June 21 — the Falkland Treaty anniversary, i.e. the show's own start date)**, residents of both Scott and Fort McMurdo gather at Hut Point to lay candles, flowers, and personal tokens — a genuinely unmandated, informal tradition, "the Saint belongs to the whole peninsula, not only to the city bearing his name." A real, low-key sibling rivalry with Fort McMurdo (Scott is "somewhat allergic to being perceived as Fort McMurdo's smaller sibling") that tracks Fort McMurdo's own historical rise to formal capital status — real texture for a recurring two-city relationship across seasons.

**Notable locations:** Hut Point / the Discovery Hut itself — explicitly proposed to survive the war intact, so it's usable as a location across the entire show's timeline including post-war epilogue material.

**Strong episode settings:** June 21 (Independence Day / Falkland Treaty anniversary) is *already* the show's own founding date — Hut Point's candlelight gathering is a ready-made recurring **season-opening or anniversary ritual episode** that could bookend multiple seasons, gaining weight as decades pass and more history accumulates under it. A "quiet domestic life next to a loud capital" contrast episode.

## JANBOGO SUBNET — CROSS-CITY SYNTHESIS (useful framing for the writers' room)

- **The entire subnet's war fate resolves to two clean principles:** cities survived either because they were "worth more alive" (Janbogo's witness/relay function, Fort McMurdo's dispersed unstrikeable footprint) or because they simply weren't worth targeting (Scott's harmlessness, Dumont d'Urville's core surviving after its junction value was disrupted) — while Zukelli and Cape Adare were destroyed for **punitive/symbolic** reasons and Denison for **pure infrastructure** reasons. This is a clean structural principle the writers can lean on: nothing in this subnet dies "randomly."
- **Janbogo's own arc across the show:** proposed to have relayed *at least two, plausibly three* separate neighboring cities' destructions (Zukelli, Cape Adare, plausibly Denison) via its highway/maritime relay role — meaning Janbogo characters carry a **cumulative, repeat-trauma witness burden**, not a single incident. A recurring "the city that has to be the one who tells everyone" role is strong material for an ensemble anchor location/family.
- **Fort McMurdo/Scott** function as a matched pair across the whole show — loud capital / quiet sibling, with a real, concrete point of collaboration (Erebus monitoring) and a real point of friction (capital-status resentment) that can evolve over decades.
- Zukelli, Cape Adare, and Denison are **all living cities for effectively the entire 248-year Southern Lights timeline** and only become ruins in 2812 — the very edge of the show's own end date. This is a gift for long-form TV: build deep affection for these places across seasons, then use their loss as a finale-adjacent gut-punch rather than backstory.

## MIRNY SUBNET ("Australian"/East Antarctic Coast, Larsemann Hills Region)

Real-world basis: the Mirny Station/Davis Coast/Larsemann Hills/East Antarctic Plateau arc — a genuinely wide span from Prydz Bay coastline to the deep interior (Southern Pole of Inaccessibility). Eight cities: Mirny (hub), Casey, Davis, Vostok, Kunlun, Zhongshan, "Soyuz" (currently unnamed — the GDD explicitly flags this as a placeholder name pending an eventual Korean rename; **use with a note that the proper name is TBD in-universe**), Shirayuki.

**War-status:** Only **Casey** is destroyed [POST-WAR, 2812]; the other seven all survive in some form (several damaged, several fully intact) — this subnet's entire war-damage logic resolves to a *single* principle (infrastructure value/reachability alone — no punitive strikes here, unlike Janbogo/Ross). That means Mirny subnet is, on the whole, a much more stable "home base" region across the show's run than Janbogo subnet — good for anchoring recurring family homes/businesses that don't get blown up.

### MIRNY — "a city built entirely as its own windbreak" (subnet hub)

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Sits precisely on the Antarctic Circle. Built as concentric, scattered rings 2–3 layers deep — a heavily fortified, smoothed outer ring specifically engineered to shed wind, wrapped around huge industrial yards where machinery runs "more often than not," with residential space folded directly into the same inner buildings (real-world parallels: Wolfsburg's factory-enclosure model, Nizhny Tagil's single-dominant-industry scale). Entertainment is minimal — rough, unpolished bars. A few days each year near each solstice, the sun grazes the horizon rather than fully rising or setting — a quiet, anticipated civic moment, visible literal proof the city sits on the polar line.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Heavy industry and construction — quarrying raw material, manufacturing both construction materials and infrastructure-maintenance machinery for the whole subnet's highway network. Genuinely balanced economy (industrial ~20%, on par with Arcanet-communications ~20% and technical/scientific work) — Mirny is a "doubled hub," carrying both the subnet's information traffic and its physical industrial output, a heavier functional load than Janbogo's culture-and-hospitality-only hub role.

**Cultural practices:** "Competence over romance" — an explicitly **rough** city by its own residents' reckoning, but with genuine, unforced pride sitting alongside the hardship, not despite it (real-world parallel: Yakutsk's plain, unromanticized endurance of extreme cold). China leads demographically with no American presence at any tier — the only Tepenian city like this; Russia (the founding/naming nation) sits only at Significant tier, and the city itself is flagged in-universe as a candidate for an eventual rename since its Russian namesake no longer matches its lived demographic reality (useful worldbuilding texture: naming as an ongoing, unresolved civic question, not a fixed fact).

**Notable locations:** The windbreak ring itself, functioning as the whole city's architecture; the industrial yards.

**Strong episode settings:** A "the machine never stops" industrial-city episode — factory floor as social space, rough bars as ensemble hangouts, contrasted against Kunlun/Vostok's cerebral quiet later in the subnet. The solstice sun-grazing-horizon moment as a recurring, visually striking seasonal beat usable across many episodes/seasons.

### ZHONGSHAN — "the one city where the founding nation never lost the top spot; two great cultures fused unplanned"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Larsemann Hills, Prydz Bay — part of a tri-junction cluster with "Soyuz" and Shirayuki (all three physically co-located at one highway junction). Reads as three architectural layers at once: utilitarian founding-era station structures at the core, aesthetically-refined exile-era construction around them, and ~130 years of outer-ring building in a mature idiom that works *with* the Larsemann Hills' exposed bedrock rather than hiding it. Streets look chaotic to a newcomer but sit on a semi-gridlike order every resident navigates unconsciously (real-world parallel: Yekaterinburg's grid-plus-organic-growth history).

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** A deep, unhurried Sino-Russian cultural synthesis nobody designed on purpose — tea-and-samovar tradition as a shared civic ritual; a cross-cultural artisan tradition concentrated in a proposed "Crossing Quarter" where craftspeople work fluently at the interface of both founding material cultures; Prydz Bay fishing (krill and Antarctic silverfish, net-fished in the brief ice-free summer, ice-hole fished in winter) with fermentation/preservation traditions drawn from both Dongbei and Russian source cuisines.

**Cultural practices:** A strong **non-prying social norm** — nobody asks personal questions; heritage is a "double life," practiced quietly at home while a shared, unified public civic rhythm governs daily life outside it. A genuine counterculture district (proposed nickname "the Loud Quarter," deliberately never given an official name — the city's non-prying instinct extended to an entire district, not formalizing what it tacitly tolerates). Political stance toward China itself: "we are more Chinese than they are; we kept the name; we did not keep them" (real-world parallel: Vilnius's fierce, layered independence from larger neighbors). A proposed defining musical work, "The Long Winter Suite" — a long, unhurried Sino-Russian classical fusion piece structured around the ~60-day polar night, using erhu/pipa/guqin in dialogue with Russian classical structure.

**Notable locations:** The Crossing Quarter (artisan neighborhood); "the Loud Quarter" (unnamed counterculture district); a proposed central rock outcrop ("the Standing Stone") as an informal gathering site.

**Strong episode settings:** A "double life" episode built around the tension/relief of a character's private heritage-practice at home vs. public civic identity outside; a slow-burn, patient episode structured around the polar night itself (mirroring "The Long Winter Suite"); the Crossing Quarter as a recurring workshop/maker-space location for craftsperson characters.

### SHIRAYUKI (白雪, "white snow") — "the city founded by treaty before the exiles even arrived; became a genuine arts-and-fashion culture"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Third city in the Larsemann Hills tri-junction cluster. Clean, orderly streets — cleanliness as active civic pride. Art is everywhere: sculptures, murals, statues, and constructed pieces in every district, densest downtown, where walking through the city center is "as much an outdoor gallery as a functioning city" — a genuine mix of civically-commissioned (downtown) and independent/grassroots (residential, informal districts) work.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Excellent schools that draw students from across Tepenia specifically for **the culture and soul of the place**, not academic ranking — a self-reinforcing loop where the amateur arts scene may have been seeded by generations of students who stayed after their studies (real-world parallel: Hongdae, Seoul). Massive amateur art-and-music-making culture running alongside genuine institutional research/education depth.

**Cultural practices:** People dress fashionably but explicitly not in a runway/high-fashion register — described as Alt-Rock/Alt-Grunge/Alt-Pop, **homemade**: assembled, altered, personalized rather than bought polished (real-world parallels: Harajuku/Shibuya street fashion, gyaru culture, Shibuya-kei music). Two proposed named local movements: **"Ashiato" fashion** (Japanese for "footprint" — visibly hand-altered garments, deliberately imperfect stitching/mismatched layering as a mark of authenticity) and **"Glacier-pop"** (a musical fusion blending Chinese/Japanese folk instrumentation with the subnet's broader multinational influences, performed in informal friends-and-neighbors spaces rather than dedicated venues). It's "genuinely, uncomplicatedly easy to make friends here." Founded via pre-exile diplomatic engineering (a deal struck at the Jeju-do Court, before the exile era even began, specifically to prevent an uncontested Chinese demographic default across the whole cluster) — an *imposed* origin that became, over generations, a genuinely *chosen* identity (real-world parallel: Akademgorodok/Novosibirsk, proof a deliberately-planned research/arts city can achieve authentic excellence, not merely imitate organic growth).

**Notable locations:** The downtown gallery-district core; informal grassroots mural/art zones in residential neighborhoods.

**Strong episode settings:** A "young person's arrival" episode — a newcomer character drawn here specifically for the culture, discovering the homemade-fashion/DIY-music scene (structurally similar to a real-world "moved to the city for the art scene" story, but with genuine sci-fi worldbuilding underneath). A street-art/mural-painting sequence as a recurring visual signature. A slow exploration of "imposed vs. chosen" identity as thematic material for a character born into circumstances they didn't choose (echoes the robot-personhood themes central to the wider setting).

### KUNLUN — "the highest, coldest, calmest point on the ice sheet; a 100%-robot city where work never stops being play"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Dome A, the East Antarctic Plateau, ~4,093m elevation — the highest city in Tepenia. A massive communications antenna towers over the whole city, flanked by satellite dishes and space-monitoring arrays — the first thing visible on approach, functioning as an unintentional civic monument. Every building is thermally sealed, interiors dominated by scientific hardware and continuous holographic data displays (spectra, signal traces, orbital telemetry) — walking the corridors means walking through "an ambient wash of live scientific visualization."

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Astronomy, deep-space communications relay, and near-Earth object monitoring, run as one enormous distributed instrument that people happen to live inside. **100% robot population** (one of only two such cities in Tepenia, with Dome Fuji) — no humans live here at all (by 2822 a deliberate, considered policy; the human population's decline during the SIP was itself gradual, tied to chronic altitude sickness). Because "the sky and the signal never stop," there is **no shared "evening"** — bars, music halls, and restaurants run 24 hours, since some fraction of the population is always coming off or going onto shift.

**Cultural practices:** "Nobody here thinks of the work as a job" — labor reframed completely as identity (real-world parallel: MIT's "mens et manus" ethos). A curated, deliberately multinational population (19 nations) selected specifically for real astronomy/space/comms credentials rather than organic settlement — converging with a religious population too: **Kunlun is the holiest site of "Ice Cold Buddhism,"** a faith that doesn't organize around nationality, so scientists-by-vocation and devotees-by-faith arrive at the same coordinates for compatible reasons. Devotees hold formal, observed periods of stillness during specific astronomical/atmospheric events, especially during the −68°C "perfect calm" of polar night — treated as devotional practice, not just a working condition to schedule around.

**Notable locations:** The antenna array itself; the observatory/comms facility that effectively *is* the city.

**Strong episode settings:** An all-robot city episode — useful precisely because your show's ageless robot characters could have a place that's entirely theirs, no humans at all, a genuinely different social register than anywhere else in the ensemble's world. A 24-hour-city episode with no day/night structure — disorienting, dreamlike blocking opportunities. A devotional-stillness sequence during polar night as a striking, near-wordless set piece.

### VOSTOK — "the coldest city on Earth, sitting above a sealed 25-million-year-old lake, inhabited by people with no ancestral claim to its own legend"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** East Antarctic Plateau, near the Southern Pole of Inaccessibility, 3,488m elevation — real-world Vostok Station is the coldest place on Earth. Huge sections of the city are laboratory space — floor after floor of genetics research organized around Lake Vostok's ancient, isolated ecosystem (sealed from the sky for 15–25 million years).

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Genetics research at a scale **too vast for any single mind — human or robot — to fully comprehend**; understanding is necessarily distributed across the whole research population (real-world parallel: NSF Materials Innovation Platforms — modern science too complex for any one lab). Humans mostly keep a day/night schedule; robots keep none at all, working until a task is finished, then socializing with whoever else happens to be between tasks — no fixed shift, no shared daily rhythm (a genuinely alien social texture worth dramatizing: two different time-cultures cohabiting one city).

**Cultural practices:** A population that **arrived by demographic accident** (a later, unrelated resettlement, decades after the original Russian founding community quietly thinned through ordinary attrition) and inherited a scientific legacy that was never theirs to build — yet largely chose to take up the Lake Vostok mission anyway, because the work itself was worth doing, not out of inherited duty. Russian survives only as institutional/technical vocabulary (equipment labels, archive terms) — like Latin in real-world science — while everyday spoken language is English/Japanese-led, matching the current population. A quiet, ongoing mutual-dependency relationship with Kunlun ("the two loneliest outposts") — equipment/reagent requests and shared research going both ways via the comms relay.

**Notable locations:** The Lake Vostok drilling/research archive itself, described as "many overlapping partial pictures, compiled by people who never coordinated with each other across centuries" — not one big unread file, but centuries of siloed, imperfectly cross-referenced data.

**Strong episode settings:** An "inherited a legend that isn't yours" character episode — a resident grappling with living inside someone else's unfinished, half-forgotten mission. A robot/human schedule-mismatch episode (two populations who are almost never awake "together" in the conventional sense, forcing asynchronous communication — notes, logs, handoffs instead of meetings). A claustrophobic, archive-labyrinth setting for a mystery/investigation storyline.

### DAVIS — "the enabler city; the most varied terrain in Tepenia, studied without needing anyone to notice"

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Vestfold Hills, Prydz Bay — genuinely unusual terrain variety within one city's footprint: exposed rock, freshwater lakes, unusual hypersaline lakes, fjord-like inlets, and the surrounding ice sheet all in the same territory (no other Tepenian city has to design around this much variety at once). Architecture is sited around lake access and fjord inlets rather than uniformly wind-defended.

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** Mining and fabrication make up the clear daily majority — Davis supplies raw materials (trucked to Zhongshan, Soyuz, etc.), functioning as the subnet's essential-but-unglamorous service/logistics base, never the flashy destination (real-world parallel: Tyumen — the unglamorous city that makes Siberian oil extraction possible without itself being the oil field). Paleoclimate research via layered lake-sediment cores (a genetics-adjacent but distinct scientific specialty from Vostok's — water as archival medium instead of ice). A modest, genuine homegrown arts/music scene, partly fed by smuggled Upper Earth books.

**Cultural practices:** Named for John King Davis, the mariner who enabled *other* men's Antarctic expeditions and never sought credit himself — the whole city's civic ethos is built around this "enabler, not protagonist" identity, reinforced independently by its namesake, its real-world inspiration (Tyumen), and its actual present-day economic structure. Help here is "genuinely useful rather than performed, precision rather than showmanship." A minority, rarely-voiced undercurrent of residents who quietly wish the Vestfold Hills' genuinely remarkable terrain drew more outside recognition (a real, if muted, internal fault line — good material for a single dissenting character rather than a citywide conflict).

**Notable locations:** The varied lake/fjord terrain itself as the city's real "landmark."

**Strong episode settings:** A quietly competent "the city that keeps everyone else running" episode — useful as a recurring supply/logistics-hub setting connecting other Mirny subnet storylines. A terrain-driven outdoor-adventure episode (hiking/boating across freshwater lakes, hypersaline lakes, and fjords in a single day's travel — visually rich and geographically distinctive). A quiet character study around the unspoken "don't we deserve some recognition" tension.

### CASEY — "the highway junction everyone passed through, home to Tepenia's most famous bar" [DESTROYED, POST-WAR 2812]

**[SIP] Physical environment / architecture:** Sits directly at the Hwy 110/Hwy 2 junction — the last coastal city before the long interior drive to Concordia. The junction itself is the immediately legible, defining geographic fact of the place — two roads meeting, a clear decision point (real-world parallel: Flagstaff, AZ).

**[SIP] Daily activities / occupations:** A city of dispatchers, road-condition reporters, and logistics coordinators — cardinal social value: "be useful, be quick, be honest about the route," since lives depend on accurate katabatic/wind-window forecasting being shared immediately. A city that's extraordinarily good at one specific, unglamorous thing: making travelers want to stay an extra night.

**Cultural practices:** **Splinters** — a full city block on the old peninsula, Tepenia's largest and most famous bar, hosting a genre-spanning Classic Rock/Jazz/Blues/Acoustic Folk music scene famous along the entire coastline (real-world parallel: Beale Street, Memphis). Its name was **deliberately rediscovered**, not invented — residents found records of a real, historical "Splinters" social bar at the pre-exile station and revived it at grand scale, a genuine, chosen act of civic self-definition (real-world parallel: Reno's 1931 pivot from mere stopover to destination). A demographically diffuse population — six nations at nearly equal Significant-tier share, no single dominant identity, because a junction city absorbed "a wider, more mixed early wave of settlers" than more isolated founding sites.

**Notable locations:** **Splinters** itself — a full city block of bar/music venue, easily one of the single best "everyone in the whole region eventually passes through here" ensemble locations available anywhere in either subnet.

**[POST-WAR, do not use as ongoing SIP fact]** Destroyed early in the Long Night War (2812) — proposed as a deliberately, specifically targeted strike, since Hwy 2 runs *through*, not just to, Casey, meaning one strike could sever two supply corridors simultaneously. Casey's destruction is framed narratively as **a transformation interrupted mid-arc** — a city moving from pure function (Flagstaff-style) toward becoming a beloved destination in its own right (Reno-style), cut off before it finished becoming what it was turning into.

**Strong episode settings:** Splinters as a recurring, cross-storyline hangout location for the entire Mirny-subnet ensemble — travelers, dispatchers, musicians, anyone passing through the region eventually shows up here, making it a natural "everyone crosses paths" set for multi-plotline episodes across many seasons. Because Casey survives almost the entire SIP and is only lost in 2812, it can function the way Zukelli does in the Janbogo subnet — deep, casual, long-running affection for the location, followed by a late-series loss that lands specifically because the show never treated it as strategically important, only beloved.

## MIRNY SUBNET — CROSS-CITY SYNTHESIS

- **A subnet of extremes bracketing a stable middle:** Kunlun (highest) and Vostok (coldest, most remote) sit at one end as nearly-otherworldly research outposts; Mirny (industrial hub), Davis (enabler), Casey (crossroads), and the Larsemann Hills cluster (Zhongshan/Soyuz/Shirayuki) sit at the more "ordinary daily life" end. This gives the writers a clean dial to turn: send an episode to the interior for something stranger and quieter, or keep it coastal/industrial for busier ensemble scenes.
- **The Larsemann Hills cluster (Zhongshan, "Soyuz," Shirayuki)** took roughly a century and a half to grow from three separately-founded communities (organic Chinese settlement / doubled dual founding / diplomatic-treaty engineering) into one genuinely intertwined tri-city neighborhood sharing a single highway junction — a slow-burn, generational "becoming neighbors" arc that's strong material if the show wants a location whose *relationships* visibly deepen across many seasons rather than staying static.
- **Vostok and Kunlun's "two loneliest outposts" relationship** is a real, ongoing infrastructure/equipment-sharing partnership, not just companionship — good texture for an occasional two-hander episode bouncing between the two remote research cities.
- Unlike Janbogo/Ross subnet, **no city here is destroyed for punitive/symbolic reasons** — only Casey falls, and only for hard strategic (chokepoint) reasons. This subnet reads overall as more stable and "survivable" across the show's 248-year span — a good place to root a family or business that persists across generations, in contrast to the more war-scarred Janbogo subnet.

## SUPER-ULTRA-MEGASHEET — WHOLE-FEDERATION PATTERNS

This document sits above every region and asks how Tepenia hangs together as a nation, not just as isolated cities. It is explicitly flagged **TENTATIVE** in the GDD (pending one unresolved DLC storyline for the Byrd region) but the patterns below are treated as settled research and are safe to use.

**Scale:** 35 cities across 6 subnets (Palmer, Halley, Janbogo/Ross, Mirny, Mawson, Byrd) plus Concordia (13 districts, the nation's last major functioning city) and Amundsen Station (a destroyed neutral inter-subnet relay). Total population ~32 million (roughly half human, half robot) at peak; post-war Antarctic surface population ~21.9 million, with ~10.1 million (about a third of all Tepenians) living in orbit by the game's present. **For the show:** orbital Tepenia exists as a demographic reality throughout the SIP and could be a location/community worth acknowledging even if the show stays surface-bound — characters with family "up there," supply runs, etc.

### Pattern: "Does the real work, gets none of the credit" is the single most repeated civic theme in the entire nation

Independently rediscovered in every region examined (Halley's Neumayer/Sanay, Janbogo's witness-burden, Mirny's Davis/Soyuz/Mirny-hub, Mawson's "connects more than it contains," Palmer's robots-as-continuity, Concordia's four-district "Recognition Cascade"). **This is Tepenia's defining national character, not a regional quirk** — for the show, it means almost any location or working-class character can plausibly carry an "invisible load-bearing competence nobody credits" throughline, and it will read as thematically consistent with the wider world rather than a one-off.

### Pattern: Robots are the nation's actual continuity mechanism — independently proven at least five separate times

Because robots live for centuries while human generations cycle every few decades, **robots are the load-bearing memory of Tepenia** — the ones who actually remember the Falkland Treaty, the founding of each city, the small human dramas of five generations ago. This is directly structural for Southern Lights' own premise (ageless robot characters anchoring a 248-year, multi-generational human cast) — the GDD's own worldbuilding independently arrived at exactly the show's own dramatic architecture. A robot character being the one who remembers "how this street used to look before your grandmother was born" is not just plausible, it's the nation's central, thematically load-bearing pattern.

### Pattern: The Long Night War was fought differently region by region — no single nationwide damage logic

Janbogo/Ross subnet needed two explanatory principles (worth-more-alive survival vs. punitive/symbolic destruction); Mirny subnet needed only one (pure infrastructure value, no punitive strikes); Halley subnet produced a four-category damage-mechanism taxonomy; Palmer subnet (the hardest-hit region in the whole game — 5 of 8 cities lost pre-war function) produced its own four-type strike doctrine plus a three-mechanism survival typology; Mawson subnet shares no single principle at all. **For the show:** this means the 2812 war should be depicted as a genuinely uneven, regionally-varied catastrophe — some places targeted with clear strategic logic, some destroyed as calculated punishment/messaging (as with Zukelli), some spared by simple remoteness or bad luck — not a single uniform event. Good material for showing the war very differently depending which of your ensemble's home cities the camera is in during the final season.

### Pattern: Local importance buys survival; *shared, nationwide* importance got Amundsen Station destroyed

Nearly every surviving city in the game survived via some version of "not worth the cost of destroying" (remoteness, marginal value, calculated restraint). The one glaring exception: **Amundsen Station**, the single most nationally load-bearing piece of *neutral, inter-subnet* infrastructure in the country, was destroyed *specifically because* of its centrality — its loss (the "Planetary Split Brain") severed inter-subnet communications nationwide, and every surface region outside its own home subnet still feels that loss in the game's present. For the show: Amundsen Station's destruction is arguably **the single most consequential event in Tepenian history after the Falkland Treaty itself** and would make an extraordinary mid/late-series event — a moment after which every subnet's ensemble is suddenly, visibly cut off from every other subnet's ensemble, breaking up the "everyone can talk to everyone" assumption the show may have relied on until then.

### Pattern: Founding-population formation never works the same way twice

At least six distinct mechanisms recur across the nation: organic multi-generational immigration, deliberate curated draws, demographic-redistribution accidents, doubled dual foundings, pre-exile diplomatic engineering, and total founding-population succession. **For the show:** don't default every new city or family background to "generic exiles settled here" — the GDD's own worldbuilding treats "how did this specific population get here" as a real, city-specific story worth individualizing, exactly the kind of texture that differentiates one location/family from the next in a large ensemble show.

### Throughway: The "Cradle" robot-manufacturing network ties directly across both of your researched subnets

A single nationwide supply chain runs: Neumayer's uncredited chamber-schematic design (Halley subnet) → active manufacturing at Byrd and at "Soyuz" (Mirny subnet, one of your two subnets) → historical (now-dark) manufacturing at Denison (Janbogo subnet, your other subnet) and at Mountain Pass (a joint Vostok-Kunlun venture, also Mirny subnet) → Mountain Pass's dependence on the Amundsen Tower's power grid → the Tower's collapse (destroying Mountain Pass's capability, though earlier-built chambers keep functioning wherever they shipped). **This means Denison and "Soyuz," specifically — both covered above — are literally part of the same physical supply chain that creates new robot citizens nationwide.** For a show anchored by ageless robot characters, this is a ready-made, already-mapped, in-universe answer to "where did this robot character's body actually come from" that ties your two researched subnets together mechanically, not just thematically.

### Throughway: A single coerced signature at the very founding still shapes institutions centuries later

Maggie Aarden, an unwilling early leader of robot resettlement, was made the robot signatory of the Falkland Treaty (June 21, 2564 — **Southern Lights' own start date**) under deliberately coercive circumstances. That coercion is the traceable, unacknowledged root of at least three present-day Concordia district institutions, and the same underlying thematic territory (a robot denied full agency, with consequences rippling outward for generations) is explicitly the inciting shape behind the Long Night War itself. **For the show:** this is a directly usable founding-moment event for a premiere episode or flashback — the Federation's entire national mythology rests on an act of coercion against a robot character that the nation's own official story has never fully reckoned with, which is thematically rich ground for a show spanning exactly the period from that signature to the war it arguably helped cause.
